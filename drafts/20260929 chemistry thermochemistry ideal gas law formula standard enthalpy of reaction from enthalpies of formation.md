### **Problem 5**

#### **Text/Statement:**

葡萄糖完全燃烧的热化学反应方程式为：


$$\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6(\text{g}) + 6\text{O}_2(\text{g}) = 6\text{CO}_2(\text{g}) + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}); \quad Q_p = -2820\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$$


当葡萄糖在人体内氧化时，上述反应热约 $30\%$ 可用作肌肉的活动能量。试估算一食匙葡萄糖（约 $3.8\text{ g}$）在人体内氧化时可获得的肌肉活动的能量。

---

#### **Solution & Explanation:**

1. **Calculate the molar mass of glucose ($\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6$):**

$$M = 6 \times 12.011 + 12 \times 1.008 + 6 \times 15.999 \approx 180.16\text{ g}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$$


2. **Calculate the amount (moles) of glucose in $3.8\text{ g}$:**

$$n = \frac{m}{M} = \frac{3.8\text{ g}}{180.16\text{ g}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}} \approx 0.02109\text{ mol}$$


3. **Calculate the total heat released ($Q_{\text{total}}$):**

$$Q_{\text{total}} = n \times \vert{}Q_p\vert{} = 0.02109\text{ mol} \times 2820\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1} \approx 59.48\text{ kJ}$$


4. **Calculate energy available for muscle activity ($E_{\text{muscle}}$):**

$$E_{\text{muscle}} = Q_{\text{total}} \times 30\% = 59.48\text{ kJ} \times 0.30 \approx 17.84\text{ kJ}$$



**Answer:**
The energy obtained for muscle activity is approximately **$17.8\text{ kJ}$** (or **$18\text{ kJ}$** depending on rounding).

### **Problem 8**

#### **Text/Statement:**

在下列反应或过程中，$Q_V$ 与 $Q_p$ 有区别吗？简单说明理由。

1. $\text{NH}_4\text{HS}(\text{s}) \xrightarrow{25^\circ\text{C}} \text{NH}_3(\text{g}) + \text{H}_2\text{S}(\text{g})$
2. $\text{H}_2(\text{g}) + \text{Cl}_2(\text{g}) \xrightarrow{25^\circ\text{C}} 2\text{HCl}(\text{g})$
3. $\text{CO}_2(\text{s}) \xrightarrow{-78^\circ\text{C}} \text{CO}_2(\text{g})$
4. $\text{AgNO}_3(\text{aq}) + \text{NaCl}(\text{aq}) \xrightarrow{25^\circ\text{C}} \text{AgCl}(\text{s}) + \text{NaNO}_3(\text{aq})$

---

#### **Solution & Explanation:**

**General Principle:**
$Q_p$ (heat at constant pressure, equal to $\Delta H$) and $Q_V$ (heat at constant volume, equal to $\Delta U$) are related by:


$$Q_p = Q_V + \Delta n_{\text{g}} R T$$


where $\Delta n_{\text{g}} = \sum n_{\text{g, products}} - \sum n_{\text{g, reactants}}$ is the change in the number of moles of gaseous substances.

* If **$\Delta n_{\text{g}} \neq 0$**, then **$Q_p \neq Q_V$** (there is a difference).
* If **$\Delta n_{\text{g}} = 0$**, then **$Q_p = Q_V$** (there is no difference).

---

1. **$\text{NH}_4\text{HS}(\text{s}) \to \text{NH}_3(\text{g}) + \text{H}_2\text{S}(\text{g})$**
* $\Delta n_{\text{g}} = (1 + 1) - 0 = +2 \neq 0$
* **Conclusion:** **Yes, there is a difference ($Q_p \neq Q_V$).**
* **Reason:** Gas is produced ($\Delta n_{\text{g}} = +2$), causing expansion work $P\Delta V \neq 0$.


2. **$\text{H}_2(\text{g}) + \text{Cl}_2(\text{g}) \to 2\text{HCl}(\text{g})$**
* $\Delta n_{\text{g}} = 2 - (1 + 1) = 0$
* **Conclusion:** **No difference ($Q_p = Q_V$).**
* **Reason:** The total number of moles of gas does not change during the reaction ($\Delta n_{\text{g}} = 0$).


3. **$\text{CO}_2(\text{s}) \to \text{CO}_2(\text{g})$**
* $\Delta n_{\text{g}} = 1 - 0 = +1 \neq 0$
* **Conclusion:** **Yes, there is a difference ($Q_p \neq Q_V$).**
* **Reason:** Solid turns into gas, producing a volume expansion ($\Delta n_{\text{g}} = +1$).


4. **$\text{AgNO}_3(\text{aq}) + \text{NaCl}(\text{aq}) \to \text{AgCl}(\text{s}) + \text{NaNO}_3(\text{aq})$**
* $\Delta n_{\text{g}} = 0 - 0 = 0$ (no gaseous reactants or products involved).
* **Conclusion:** **No significant difference ($Q_p \approx Q_V$).**
* **Reason:** Only condensed phases (liquid solutions and solid precipitate) are involved, so volume change is negligibly small.

### **Problem 10**

#### **Text/Statement:**

查阅附录3的数据，试计算下列反应的 $\Delta_{\text{r}}H_m^\ominus(298.15\text{ K})$：
(1) $4\text{NH}_3(\text{g}) + 3\text{O}_2(\text{g}) = 2\text{N}_2(\text{g}) + 6\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l})$

---

#### **Solution & Explanation:**

**1. Standard Enthalpies of Formation ($\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus$ at $298.15\text{ K}$) from standard thermodynamic tables:**

* $\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus[\text{NH}_3(\text{g})] = -45.9\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$
* $\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus[\text{O}_2(\text{g})] = 0\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ (element in standard state)
* $\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus[\text{N}_2(\text{g})] = 0\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ (element in standard state)
* $\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus[\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l})] = -285.83\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$

**2. Formula:**


$$\Delta_{\text{r}}H_m^\ominus = \sum \nu_i \Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus(\text{products}) - \sum \nu_j \Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus(\text{reactants})$$

**3. Calculation:**


$$\begin{aligned} \Delta_{\text{r}}H_m^\ominus &= \left[ 2 \times \Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus(\text{N}_2, \text{g}) + 6 \times \Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus(\text{H}_2\text{O}, \text{l}) \right] - \left[ 4 \times \Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus(\text{NH}_3, \text{g}) + 3 \times \Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus(\text{O}_2, \text{g}) \right] \\ &= \left[ 2(0) + 6(-285.83) \right] - \left[ 4(-45.9) + 3(0) \right] \\ &= -1714.98 - (-183.6) \\ &= -1531.38\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1} \end{aligned}$$

**Answer:**


$$\Delta_{\text{r}}H_m^\ominus(298.15\text{ K}) \approx -1531.4\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$$

### **Problem 15**

#### **Text/Statement:**

通过吸收气体中所含的少量乙醇可使 $\text{K}_2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7$ 酸性溶液变色（从橙红色变为绿色），以检验汽车驾驶员是否酒后驾车（违反交通规则）。其化学反应可表示为：


$$2\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}(\text{aq}) + 16\text{H}^+(\text{aq}) + 3\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}(\text{l}) = 4\text{Cr}^{3+}(\text{aq}) + 11\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l}) + 3\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}(\text{l})$$


试利用标准摩尔生成焓数据，求该反应的 $\Delta_{\text{r}}H_m^\ominus(298.15\text{ K})$。

---

#### **Solution & Explanation:**

**1. Standard Enthalpies of Formation ($\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus$ at $298.15\text{ K}$) from standard tables:**

* $\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus[\text{Cr}_2\text{O}_7^{2-}(\text{aq})] = -1490.3\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$
* $\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus[\text{H}^+(\text{aq})] = 0\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$ (by definition)
* $\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus[\text{C}_2\text{H}_5\text{OH}(\text{l})] = -277.69\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$
* $\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus[\text{Cr}^{3+}(\text{aq})] = -205.0\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$
* $\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus[\text{H}_2\text{O}(\text{l})] = -285.83\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$
* $\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus[\text{CH}_3\text{COOH}(\text{l})] = -484.5\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$

**2. Formula:**


$$\Delta_{\text{r}}H_m^\ominus = \sum_{\text{products}} \nu_i \Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus - \sum_{\text{reactants}} \nu_j \Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus$$

**3. Calculation:**

* **Products:**

$$\begin{aligned}   \sum \Delta_{\text{f}}H_{\text{products}} &= 4 \times (-205.0) + 11 \times (-285.83) + 3 \times (-484.5) \\   &= -820.0 - 3144.13 - 1453.5 \\   &= -5417.63\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}   \end{aligned}$$

* **Reaction Enthalpy:**

$$\begin{aligned}   \Delta_{\text{r}}H_m^\ominus &= -5417.63 - (-3813.67) \\   &= -1603.96\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}   \end{aligned}$$

$$\Delta_{\text{r}}H_m^\ominus(298.15\text{ K}) \approx -1604\text{ kJ}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$$




# Explain the formulas 

Here is an explanation of the core thermodynamic formulas used to solve the problems above:

---

### **1. Ideal Gas Expansion Work and $Q_p$ vs. $Q_V$ Relationship**

$$Q_p = Q_V + \Delta n_{\text{g}} R T$$

* **$Q_p$ (Heat at Constant Pressure):** The heat exchanged when a reaction takes place under constant pressure. Equal to the enthalpy change $\Delta H$.
* **$Q_V$ (Heat at Constant Volume):** The heat exchanged when a reaction takes place inside a rigid container where volume cannot change. Equal to the internal energy change $\Delta U$.
* **$\Delta n_{\text{g}}$ (Change in Moles of Gas):**

$$\Delta n_{\text{g}} = \sum n_{\text{g, products}} - \sum n_{\text{g, reactants}}$$



This represents the net change in the number of moles of gaseous substances during the reaction.
* **$R$:** The ideal gas constant ($8.314\text{ J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$).
* **$T$:** Absolute temperature in Kelvin ($\text{K}$).

**Physical Meaning:**
When a reaction occurs at constant pressure and produces gas ($\Delta n_{\text{g}} > 0$), the system expands against the surrounding atmosphere, doing pressure-volume work ($W = P\Delta V = \Delta n_{\text{g}}RT$). Thus, additional heat ($Q_p$) must be supplied or released compared to when the reaction is confined to a fixed volume ($Q_V$). If no gas is produced or consumed ($\Delta n_{\text{g}} = 0$), no expansion work is done, so $Q_p = Q_V$.

---

### **2. Standard Enthalpy of Reaction from Enthalpies of Formation**

$$\Delta_{\text{r}}H_m^\ominus = \sum \nu_i \Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus(\text{products}) - \sum \nu_j \Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus(\text{reactants})$$

* **$\Delta_{\text{r}}H_m^\ominus$ (Standard Molar Enthalpy of Reaction):** The total enthalpy change when reactants in their standard states are converted completely into products in their standard states at temperature $T$.
* **$\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus$ (Standard Molar Enthalpy of Formation):** The enthalpy change that accompanies the formation of $1\text{ mol}$ of a chemical compound from its constituent elements in their standard reference states. By definition, $\Delta_{\text{f}}H_m^\ominus = 0$ for any element in its standard state (e.g., $\text{O}_2(\text{g})$, $\text{N}_2(\text{g})$, $\text{H}^+(\text{aq})$).
* **$\nu$ (Stoichiometric Coefficient):** The number of moles of each substance appearing in the balanced chemical equation.

**Physical Meaning:**
According to Hess's Law, enthalpy is a state function—its value depends only on the initial and final states of the system, not on the path taken. Calculating the reaction enthalpy is equivalent to breaking down all reactants into their basic elements and then reassembling those elements to form the products.

---

### **3. Heat and Mass Calculations**

$$n = \frac{m}{M}$$

* **$n$:** Amount of substance in moles ($\text{mol}$).
* **$m$:** Mass of the sample in grams ($\text{g}$).
* **$M$:** Molar mass of the substance in grams per mole ($\text{g}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}$).

$$Q_{\text{total}} = n \times \vert{}\Delta_{\text{r}}H_m^\ominus\vert{}$$

* **$Q_{\text{total}}$:** Total heat released or absorbed by a specific mass of reactant.
* **$\vert{}\Delta_{\text{r}}H_m^\ominus\vert{}$:** Absolute value of the standard molar enthalpy change per mole of reaction.

# Explain pv formula

The $PV$ formula refers to the **Pressure–Volume ($PV$) Work** formula in thermodynamics.

---

### **1. The Formula**

$$W = -\int P_{\text{ext}} \, dV$$

For a process occurring against a **constant external pressure** ($P_{\text{ext}}$), it simplifies to:

$$W = -P_{\text{ext}} \Delta V$$

Where:

* **$W$** = Work done on or by the system (in Joules, $\text{J}$).
* **$P_{\text{ext}}$** = External pressure opposing the expansion/compression (in Pascals, $\text{Pa}$, or $\text{N/m}^2$).
* **$\Delta V$** = Change in volume, defined as $V_{\text{final}} - V_{\text{initial}}$ (in cubic meters, $\text{m}^3$, or liters, $\text{L}$).

---

### **2. Physical Meaning**

When a fluid (such as a gas in a cylinder with a piston) expands or compresses, it moves a boundary against an external force.

* **Expansion ($\Delta V > 0$):** The gas expands and pushes the piston outward. The gas does work on the surroundings. Because of the negative sign in $W = -P \Delta V$, $W < 0$, meaning the system loses energy as work.
* **Compression ($\Delta V < 0$):** An external force pushes the piston inward, compressing the gas. Work is done *on* the system. Here, $W > 0$, meaning energy is transferred into the system.
* **Constant Volume ($\Delta V = 0$):** If the volume does not change (a rigid container), no $PV$ work is done ($W = 0$).

---

### **3. For Ideal Gases: $PV = nRT$**

In ideal gas systems, $PV$ work is directly tied to the **Ideal Gas Law**:

$$P V = n R T$$

When an ideal gas reaction or phase change occurs at constant temperature ($T$) and pressure ($P$), a change in the amount of gas ($\Delta n_{\text{g}}$) causes a change in volume ($\Delta V$):

$$P \Delta V = \Delta n_{\text{g}} R T$$

This links $PV$ work directly to chemical reaction thermodynamics:

* **Internal Energy Change ($\Delta U$):** Energy change at constant volume ($Q_V$).
* **Enthalpy Change ($\Delta H$):** Energy change at constant pressure ($Q_p$).
* **Relationship:** $\Delta H = \Delta U + P\Delta V = \Delta U + \Delta n_{\text{g}} RT$.

# Explain ideal gas pv formula

The **Ideal Gas Law** links the four fundamental macroscopic properties of an ideal gas—Pressure ($P$), Volume ($V$), Amount of substance ($n$), and Temperature ($T$)—into a single equation:

$$PV = nRT$$

---

### **1. Breakdown of the Variables**

* **$P$ (Pressure):** The force per unit area exerted by gas molecules colliding with the walls of their container (measured in Pascals $\text{Pa}$, atmospheres $\text{atm}$, or $\text{kPa}$).
* **$V$ (Volume):** The space occupied by the gas (measured in cubic meters $\text{m}^3$ or liters $\text{L}$).
* **$n$ (Amount of Gas):** The quantity of gas measured in moles ($\text{mol}$).
* **$R$ (Ideal Gas Constant):** The proportionality constant that bridges the units. Its value depends on the units used for $P$ and $V$:
* $R = 8.314\text{ J}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$ (when using $\text{Pa}$ and $\text{m}^3$, or $\text{kPa}$ and $\text{L}$)
* $R = 0.08206\text{ L}\cdot\text{atm}\cdot\text{mol}^{-1}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$ (when using $\text{atm}$ and $\text{L}$)


* **$T$ (Absolute Temperature):** The thermal energy of the gas measured in **Kelvin ($\text{K}$)** ($\text{K} = {^\circ\text{C}} + 273.15$).

---

### **2. Derivation from Individual Gas Laws**

The Ideal Gas Law combines three empirical gas laws established by early chemists:

1. **Boyle’s Law:** $V \propto \frac{1}{P}$ (at constant $n, T$) — Volume is inversely proportional to pressure.
2. **Charles’s Law:** $V \propto T$ (at constant $n, P$) — Volume is directly proportional to absolute temperature.
3. **Avogadro’s Law:** $V \propto n$ (at constant $P, T$) — Volume is directly proportional to the number of moles.

Combining these three proportions gives:

$$V \propto \frac{n T}{P} \implies PV \propto n T \implies PV = nRT$$

---

### **3. What Makes a Gas "Ideal"?**

An **ideal gas** is a theoretical model that assumes:

1. **Zero Molecular Volume:** Gas particles themselves take up no physical space compared to the total volume of the container.
2. **No Intermolecular Forces:** There are no attractive or repulsive forces between the particles.
3. **Elastic Collisions:** Energy is perfectly conserved when particles collide with each other or container walls.

While no real gas is perfectly ideal, most real gases (like $\text{O}_2$, $\text{N}_2$, $\text{H}_2$) behave nearly ideally under conditions of **high temperature** and **low pressure**.

---

### **4. Useful Rearrangements**

* **Finding Density ($\rho$):**
Since $n = \frac{m}{M}$ (where $m$ is mass and $M$ is molar mass):

$$PV = \frac{m}{M}RT \implies \rho = \frac{m}{V} = \frac{P M}{R T}$$


* **Combined Gas Law (for a fixed amount of gas, constant $n$):**

$$\frac{P_1 V_1}{T_1} = \frac{P_2 V_2}{T_2}$$

# Why

Why does the Ideal Gas Law ($PV = nRT$) work, and why do gases behave this way?

It boils down to **Kinetic Molecular Theory**, which views a gas as a massive collection of tiny particles (atoms or molecules) in constant, random motion.

---

### **1. Why Pressure ($P$) exists**

Gas molecules constantly bounce off the walls of their container. Every time a molecule hits a wall, it exerts a tiny force. Billions of these collisions happening every second create a steady force over the surface area, which we measure as **pressure** ($P = \frac{\text{Force}}{\text{Area}}$).

---

### **2. Why Temperature ($T$) matters**

Temperature is a direct measure of the **average kinetic energy** of the gas molecules ($\text{KE} = \frac{1}{2}m v^2$).

* Higher temperature $\implies$ Molecules move faster $\implies$ They hit the container walls harder and more frequently $\implies$ Higher pressure or larger volume.

---

### **3. Why the relationship $PV = nRT$ holds**

* **Why $P \propto \frac{1}{V}$ (Boyle's Law):**
If you squeeze a gas into half its volume, the same number of molecules now occupy less space. They will collide with the walls twice as often, doubling the pressure.
* **Why $V \propto T$ (Charles's Law):**
If you heat a gas, the molecules speed up and hit the container walls harder. If the walls can expand (like a balloon), the volume must increase to keep the pressure balanced.
* **Why $V \propto n$ (Avogadro's Law):**
Adding more molecules means more collisions, requiring more space (volume) if the pressure and temperature remain constant.

When you put these three physical behaviors together mathematically, you get $PV \propto nT$, which becomes **$PV = nRT$** using $R$ as the constant factor to balance the units.
