adjective to describe a ui full of interactive elements

The choice of adjective depends on whether the high amount of interactivity is a positive feature or a negative flaw.

### Positive Adjectives (Engaging & Responsive)

- **Dynamic**: Implies the interface is lively, constantly changing, and reacts fluidly to inputs.
- **Immersive**: Suggests the heavy interaction deeply pulls users into the experience.
- **Feature-rich**: Focuses on the sheer utility and the vast capabilities available to the user.
- **Highly tactile**: Describes a UI that feels responsive and physical, often packed with micro-interactions.
- **Manipulable**: Emphasizes that almost every element on the screen can be changed, dragged, or adjusted by the user. [link](https://www.uxdesigninstitute.com/blog/ui-glossary/)

### Neutral Adjectives (Functional Description)

- **High-density**: Describes a screen packed with a massive amount of controls and data views.
- **Control-heavy**: Identifies that the screen is dominated by buttons, toggles, inputs, and sliders.
- **Interactive-dense**: Directly states that the layout is physically crowded with interactive components. [link](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/ui-elements-glossary/)

### Negative Adjectives (Overwhelming & Confusing)

- **Cluttered**: Indicates that the excessive options have ruined the visual hierarchy and negative space.
- **Overwhelming**: Implies the heavy interactivity causes cognitive fatigue for the user.
- **Busy**: Suggests there is too much happening visually, making it hard to focus. [link](https://uxplanet.org/50-design-styles-every-designer-should-know-for-better-prompting-56c09d55db62)
