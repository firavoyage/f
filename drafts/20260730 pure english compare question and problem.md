compare question and problem

<mark><span>A<span> </span></span><strong>question</strong><span> </span>is an inquiry seeking information, while a<span> </span><strong>problem</strong><span> </span>is a situation that requires a solution</mark>.

### Core Differences

- **Definition**: A question asks for knowledge. A problem presents a difficulty.
- **Goal**: The goal of a question is to know. The goal of a problem is to resolve.
- **Nature**: Questions are conceptual or linguistic. Problems are practical or situational.
- **Output**: A question ends with an answer. A problem ends with a solution.

### Key Characteristics

#### Question

- Seeks to fill a gap in understanding.
- Solved by providing facts, data, or explanations.
- Example: "What is the capital of France?" \[1]

#### Problem

- Seeks to overcome an obstacle or unwanted state.
- Solved by taking action, making decisions, or designing systems.
- Example: "The city's traffic congestion is delaying emergency vehicles." \[1]

### How They Interact

Problems often generate questions to help find a solution. For example, if your car won't start (a **problem**), you ask, "Is the battery dead?" (a **question**) to help fix it.

## can they overlap

Yes, questions and problems overlap frequently, especially in academic, mathematical, and technical contexts where the two terms are used interchangeably.

### Ways They Overlap

- **Academic Tasks**: A math "problem" on a test is presented as a "question" you must answer.
- **Research Focus**: A "research problem" is an issue in a field, but it is formulated as a "research question."
- **Interdependence**: A problem cannot be solved without answering questions, and a deep question often reveals a hidden problem.

### The Double-Duty Zone

- **The Prompt**: "How do we reduce carbon emissions by 20%?"
- **As a Question**: It asks for an explanation of methods and data.
- **As a Problem**: It requires political action, engineering, and resource allocation to fix.
