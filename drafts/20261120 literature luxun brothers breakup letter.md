### 鲁迅 以后请不要到后边院子来 信件

这句话出自**1923年7月18日周作人写给鲁迅的“绝交信”**，是著名的“周氏兄弟失和”事件的直接导火索与标志。 [](https://news.nankai.edu.cn/mtnk/system/2016/05/02/000279677.shtml)

#### 信件全文

> **鲁迅先生：**
> 
> 我昨天才知道，——但过去的事不必说了。我不是基督徒，却幸而尚能担受得起，也不想责谁，——大家都是可怜的人间。我以前的蔷薇的梦原来都是虚幻，现在所见的或者才是真的人生。我想订正我的思想，重新入新的生活。**以后请不要再到后边院子里来，没有别的话。愿你安心，自重。**
> 
> 七月十八日，作人。 [](https://news.nankai.edu.cn/mtnk/system/2016/05/02/000279677.shtml)

---

##### 事件始末与背景

* **递交经过。** 1923年7月19日上午，周作人将此信交予前院的鲁迅。鲁迅深感意外并试图当面询问，但遭周作人拒绝。鲁迅当天在日记中记录了这一过程。
* **居住格局。** 兄弟二人当时同住北京八道湾大院（鲁迅居前院，周作人居后院），“不要再到后边院子里来”标志着两人的正式断交。
* **直接后续。** 收到信后鲁迅迅速寻找新住所，并于同年8月2日携夫人搬离八道湾，兄弟二人从此终身未再相见。 [](https://www.chinanews.com.cn/cul/news/2007/11-20/1082405.shtml)

---

* **经济与家务矛盾。** 大家庭开销由羽太信子把持导致鲁迅长期负债，且在绝交前不久鲁迅刚改为分餐自炊，长期积压的经济与生活摩擦总爆发。

### what does these words mean

The phrase **"以后请不要再到后边院子里来" (Yǐhòu qǐng bùyào zài dào hòubiān yuànzi lǐ lái)** translates literally to: **"Please do not come to the back courtyard anymore from now on."**

In the context of Chinese literary history, it is a polite yet icy **declaration of estrangement** (a "break-up" letter between brothers).

#### Breakdown & Tone

* **Literal Meaning:** At the time (1923), Lu Xun and his younger brother Zhou Zuoren lived in the same traditional Beijing courtyard compound (_siheyuan_). Lu Xun lived in the front courtyard, while Zhou Zuoren lived with his family in the back courtyard. Saying "don't come to the back courtyard" meant **cutting off all daily contact and access to his family.**
* **Emotional Tone:** Despite the cold message, the wording uses polite markers like **"请" (please)** and **"先生" (Mr./Sir)** at the start of the letter. Calling one's own brother "Mr. Lu Xun" and politely asking him not to visit creates an atmosphere of **extreme formality, distance, and finality**—far more cutting than an angry outburst.

#### Full Letter Translation

To give you the full picture of what Zhou Zuoren wrote on July 18, 1923:

> **Mr. Lu Xun:**
> 
> I only found out yesterday—but there is no need to speak of the past. I am not a Christian, yet fortunately I am still able to bear it, and I do not wish to blame anyone—we are all pitiful human beings. My previous "rose-colored dreams" were all illusions; what I see now is perhaps the true reality of life. I want to revise my thoughts and enter a new life. **Please do not come to the back courtyard anymore from now on; there is nothing more to say. May you be at peace and take care of yourself.**
> 
> July 18, Zuoren.

### what do the specific chinese words in the letter mean

Here is a word-by-word and phrase-by-phrase breakdown of the letter's key Chinese terms to show the exact nuance and literary tone Zhou Zuoren used:

#### 1\. Salutation & Opening

* **鲁迅先生 (Lǔ Xùn xiānshēng):** **Mr. Lu Xun.** Calling an older brother "Mr." (_xiānshēng_) using his pen name instead of "Older Brother" (_dàgē_) immediately strips away all family intimacy.
* **我昨天才知道 (Wǒ zuótiān cái zhīdào):** **I only found out yesterday.** The word **才 (cái)** adds emphasis ("only just now" or "not until"), implying he had been kept in the dark.
* **过去的事不必说了 (Guòqù de shì bùbì shuō le):** **There is no need to speak of past matters.** **不必 (bùbì)** means "unnecessary," shutting down any chance for explanation or defense.

#### 2\. The Philosophical Shift

* **担受得起 (Dānshòu de qǐ):** **Able to bear/endure it.** **担受 (dānshòu)** means to shoulder a heavy burden or emotional blow; **得起 (de qǐ)** indicates having the strength to withstand it.
* **可怜的人间 (Kělián de rénjiān):** **Pitiful human world / humanity.** **可怜 (kělián)** means pitiful or pathetic. **人间 (rénjiān)** literally means "among humans," referring to the mortal world and human condition rather than just individuals.
* **蔷薇的梦 (Qiángwēi de mèng):** **Dream of roses (rose-colored dreams).** **蔷薇 (qiángwēi)** is a climbing rose. In early 20th-century Chinese literature, it symbolized romantic idealism, beauty, and utopian hope—specifically the brothers' shared ideal of a harmonious, artistic family life.
* **虚幻 (Xūhuàn):** **Illusory / unreal.** Something empty and imaginary that has shattered.
* **订正我的思想 (Dìngzhèng wǒ de sīxiǎng):** **Revise/correct my thinking.** **订正 (dìngzhèng)** is a formal term usually used for proofreading or correcting errors in a book, treating his past ideals like a mistake that needs editing.

#### 3\. The Core Rejection & Closing

* **以后 (Yǐhòu):** **From now on / in the future.**
* **请不要再 (Qǐng bùyào zài):** **Please do not \[do something\] again.** **请 (qǐng)** is "please," **不要 (bùyào)** is "don't," and **再 (zài)** means "again/anymore."
* **到后边院子里来 (Dào hòubiān yuànzi lǐ lái):** **Come into the back courtyard.** **后边 (hòubiān)** means back/rear, and **院子 (yuànzi)** is courtyard.
* **没有别的话 (Méiyǒu bié de huà):** **There are no other words / nothing else to say.** A blunt conversational marker signaling the end of the discussion.
* **愿你安心，自重 (Yuàn nǐ ānxīn, zìzhòng):** **Wish you peace of mind, and take care of yourself.**
  * **安心 (ānxīn):** Literally "settle the heart"—meaning to be at peace or not worry.
  * **自重 (zìzhòng):** Literally "self-weight/self-value." In formal Chinese farewells, it means "take care of your health and conduct yourself with dignity." Depending on tone, it can also carry a faint undertone of "watch your behavior."
