# perfectionism

**Perfectionism** is <mark>a multi-dimensional personality trait characterized by an relentless striving for flawlessness, setting impossibly high performance standards, and engaging in overly critical self-evaluations</mark>. While healthy ambition drives people to grow, unhealthy perfectionism functions as a defense mechanism against the fear of failure and rejection. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)

### The Three Core Types

Psychologists break perfectionism down into three primary dimensions based on where the pressure originates:

- **Self-Oriented**: Imposing strict, unrealistic standards on yourself and tying your self-worth directly to your achievements. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)
- **Other-Oriented**: Holding people around you (like coworkers, partners, or children) to impossible standards and becoming easily frustrated by their flaws. [link](https://stressandresilience.com/5-styles-of-perfectionism/)
- **Socially Prescribed**: Believing that society, family, or peers demand absolute perfection from you and that you will be rejected if you fall short. [link](https://www.verywellmind.com/signs-you-may-be-a-perfectionist-3145233)

### Common Signs and Hidden Traps

Perfectionism rarely looks like simple neatness. It often manifests as painful behavioral paradoxes:

- **The Procrastination Loop**: Delaying tasks out of a deep fear that the output will not meet your high standards.
- **All-or-Nothing Thinking**: Viewing life in black-and-white terms where a single mistake renders an entire project a total failure.
- **Fixation on Flaws**: Spending excessive time checking work, organizing, or correcting tiny errors while ignoring major successes.
- **Defensiveness**: Interpreting constructive feedback as a devastating personal attack on your capability. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)

### Healthy Excellence vs. Unhealthy Perfectionism

According to clinical insights from [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism), there is a vast difference between healthy achievement and maladaptive perfectionism. Healthy high-achievers focus on the process of growth, accept setbacks as learning experiences, and maintain stable self-worth. Unhealthy perfectionists focus entirely on avoiding mistakes, operate out of fear, and let their inner critic destroy their self-esteem. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)

### How to Break the Cycle

Overcoming these habits requires retraining how you view performance and worth:

1. **Practice "Good Enough"**: Intentionally finish a low-stakes task to 80% completion to prove that the world does not end when things are imperfect.
2. **Shift to Progress Goals**: Reframe your goals around effort (e.g., "I will write for 30 minutes") rather than strict outcomes ("I must write a perfect paragraph"). [link](https://www.verywellmind.com/signs-you-may-be-a-perfectionist-3145233)
3. **Challenge the Inner Critic**: Replace harsh self-talk with compassionate, objective framing—treat yourself like you would treat a friend who made a mistake. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)

# procrastination

**Procrastination is fundamentally an emotional regulation problem, not a time management failure.** It is <mark>the voluntary delay of an intended task despite knowing that the delay will cause negative consequences</mark>. Instead of laziness, it is a coping mechanism where your brain prioritizes immediate mood repair over long-term benefits. [link](https://deconstructingstigma.org/guides/procrastination)

### The Psychology: Why We Do It

Your brain experiences a literal tug-of-war when facing an unpleasant, boring, or overwhelming task: [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/procrastination)

- **The Limbic System**: This is the ancient, emotional part of your brain. It demands immediate gratification, safety, and comfort.
- **The Prefrontal Cortex**: This is the logical, rational planning center. It knows you need to finish the task to avoid future stress. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2IV3bZUk8p8\&t=107)

When a task triggers negative emotions like anxiety, self-doubt, or boredom, the limbic system treats the task as a threat. It triggers a fight-or-flight response, pushing you to escape the discomfort by scrolling social media, cleaning, or organizing instead. This avoidance provides instant relief, rewarding your brain and reinforcing a destructive habit loop. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=JssA1fkgAJY)

### Common Root Causes

Procrastination usually stems from deep-seated psychological drivers: [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/procrastination)

- **Perfectionism**: The fear that your work won't meet high standards causes you to delay starting at all.
- **Task Overwhelm**: When a project is massive or poorly defined, your brain doesn't know where to begin and freezes.
- **Present Bias**: The human tendency to value immediate rewards over distant future consequences.
- **Fear of Failure**: Avoiding a task protects your self-esteem, letting you blame a poor result on a lack of time rather than a lack of ability. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/procrastination)

### Strategic Solutions to Break the Loop

To beat procrastination, you must lower the barrier to entry and manage the emotional charge of the task. [link](https://drjud.com/procrastination/procrastination-not-laziness/)

- **The 2-Minute Rule**: Tell yourself you only have to work on the task for two minutes. Starting is the hardest part; building momentum naturally keeps you going. [link](https://jamesclear.com/procrastination)
- **Micro-Step Breakdown**: Divide a massive project into absurdly small, specific actions. Instead of "Write research paper," make the task "Open a blank document and type three bullet points". [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Procrastination)
- **Temptation Bundling**: Pair a task you avoid with something you love. For example, only listen to your favorite podcast while folding laundry or doing data entry. [link](https://jamesclear.com/procrastination)
- **Practice Self-Forgiveness**: Beating yourself up for past procrastination actually creates _more_ stress, making you more likely to procrastinate again. Cultivating self-compassion helps break the cycle. [link](https://deconstructingstigma.org/guides/procrastination)
- **Environmental Control**: Remove distractions before you sit down. Put your phone in another room or block distracting websites using tools mentioned in productivity guides like [James Clear's Procrastination Guide](https://jamesclear.com/procrastination).

# perfectionism psychology

## perfectionism psychology

In psychology, **perfectionism** is <mark>a broad multidimensional personality trait characterized by a person's intense drive for flawlessness, setting exceedingly high performance standards, and engaging in overly critical self-evaluations</mark>. Rather than just a healthy desire to do well, clinical frameworks view perfectionism as a complex psychological coping mechanism or mindset where an individual's core sense of self-worth is dangerously fused with their achievements. [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perfectionism%5F%28psychology%29)

### The Core Dimensions of Perfectionism

According to prominent clinical research by [Hewitt and Flett](https://cpa.ca/psychology-works-fact-sheet-perfectionism/), perfectionism generally manifests across three primary interpersonal domains: [link](https://cpa.ca/psychology-works-fact-sheet-perfectionism/)

- **Self-Oriented Perfectionism**: Demanding rigid, flawless standards from oneself. Individuals strictly monitor their own behaviors and filter their daily life through a harsh lens of self-criticism. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)
- **Socially Prescribed Perfectionism**: The distinct perception that society, parents, or peers hold unrealistic expectations for you. Individuals believe they will only receive acceptance or love if they achieve perfection. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)
- **Other-Oriented Perfectionism**: Projecting unrealistic expectations onto surrounding people. This involves holding friends, children, spouses, or coworkers to unyielding standards, which often causes relational conflict. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)

### Adaptive vs. Maladaptive Striving

Psychologists separate the trait into two distinct functional categories based on how it impacts an individual's well-being: [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)

| Psychological Feature    | Adaptive Perfectionism (Striving for Excellence)                      | Maladaptive Perfectionism (Problematic Striving)                               |
| ------------------------ | --------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Primary Motivation**   | Driven by a desire for personal growth, achievement, and fulfillment. | Driven entirely by a deep-seated fear of failure and judgment.                 |
| **Response to Mistakes** | Sees errors as natural learning opportunities and moves forward.      | Experiences intense emotional distress, rumination, and self-blame.            |
| **Standards & Rules**    | High goals that remain flexible according to life circumstances.      | Rigid, unyielding rules; anything short of flawless is deemed a total failure. |
| **Mental Health Impact** | Linked to healthy self-esteem, focus, and life satisfaction.          | Breeding ground for severe anxiety, chronic stress, and clinical burnout.      |

### Behavioral Signs and Symptoms

While perfectionists are often viewed outwardly as orderly or highly successful, interior psychological processes tell a different story. Common manifestations outlined by [Psychology Today](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism) include: [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)

- **Chronic Procrastination**: Delaying the start of projects due to a paralyzing fear that the final product won't match an impossible ideal. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)
- **All-or-Nothing Thinking**: Cognitive distortions where outcomes are categorized strictly as a perfect success or a catastrophic failure. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/blog/perfectionism/202508/perfectionism-and-the-excessive-need-for-control)
- **Inability to Celebrate**: Dismissing genuine accomplishments, shrugging off compliments, and instantly looking forward to the next benchmark to seek fleeting validation. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)
- **Hyper-Fixation on Details**: Getting trapped in the minutiae of everyday tasks, which actively kills efficiency and limits broader creativity. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=k6K57ox5Owc\&vl=en)

### Root Causes in Psychological Theory

Psychological literature emphasizes that perfectionism rarely develops in a vacuum. It is commonly triggered by: [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kBDuSZ0vgaw)

1. **Childhood Conditioning**: Growing up under conditional approval, where parental love or warmth felt dependent on academic, artistic, or behavioral achievements. [link](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/perfectionism-psychology)
2. **The Need for Control**: Striving for external flaws can serve as an elegant, childhood-derived psychological shield to mask deeper vulnerabilities, internal shame, or feelings of unlovability. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3mEbECK95EM)
3. **Societal Pressure**: Modern generation trends tracked by organisations like the [American Psychological Association (APA)](https://www.apa.org/monitor/2024/10/antidote-achievement-culture) show massive increases in perfectionism due to hyper-competitive educational metrics and social media performance comparisons. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)

While perfectionism is not classified as a distinct mental illness, it acts as a significant risk factor that frequently co-occurs with Generalized Anxiety Disorder (GAD), Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD), eating disorders, and clinical depression. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)

### Therapeutic Strategies to Manage Perfectionism

Perfectionism is a deeply ingrained personality style, but the cognitive loops can be unlearned through focused behavioral adjustments: [link](https://www.ebsco.com/research-starters/health-and-medicine/perfectionism-psychology)

- **Cognitive Reframing**: Learning to decouple personal human worth from objective achievement metrics.
- **Practicing Self-Compassion**: Cultivating balanced self-talk that grants oneself grace when encountering personal limitations or unexpected setbacks.
- **Behavioral Experiments**: Intentionally leaving a minor task slightly incomplete or "imperfect" to train the nervous system to realize that minor mistakes do not yield catastrophic outcomes. [link](https://www.psychologytoday.com/us/basics/perfectionism)

## causes

The causes of perfectionism stem from a complex interaction of genetic, parental, social, and psychological factors.

### 1. Childhood and Parental Factors

Family environment is one of the most prominent breeding grounds for perfectionist traits:

- **Conditional Love**: Children receive affection and praise only when they achieve high grades, win competitions, or behave flawlessly.
- **Overly Critical Parenting**: Growing up with parents who fixate on mistakes rather than celebrating progress teaches children that errors are unacceptable.
- **Modeling Perfectionism**: Children copy the behavior of parents who are highly self-critical or obsess over their own flaws.
- **Unstable Environments**: In chaotic or unpredictable homes, a child may use perfect behavior as a psychological tool to create safety and control.

### 2. Modern Societal and Cultural Drivers

Societal pressures have accelerated perfectionism rates in recent generations:

- **Academic Culture**: Schools and universities place intense focus on flawless grade point averages, test scores, and resumes.
- **Social Media Ideals**: Platforms present curated, edited feeds that pressure individuals to maintain perfect bodies, careers, and lifestyles.
- **Economic Pressure**: A hyper-competitive job market convinces people that any career misstep will result in financial failure.

### 3. Psychological and Emotional Triggers

Internal defense mechanisms often drive perfectionist loops:

- **Low Self-Worth**: People fuse their personal value with their performance, believing they are only worthy if they are flawless.
- **Fear of Rejection**: Individuals believe that making a mistake will cause friends, family, or peers to abandon them.
- **Anxiety and Control**: Perfectionism functions as an emotional shield to mask deep-seated feelings of vulnerability, helplessness, or shame.

### 4. Biological and Genetic Influences

Research shows that some underlying biological traits make people more vulnerable to perfectionism:

- **Heritable Personality Traits**: High levels of neuroticism (emotional reactivity) and conscientiousness are frequently passed down genetically.
- **Neurological Ties**: Perfectionism shares genetic pathways with mental health conditions like Obsessive-Compulsive Disorder (OCD) and anxiety.

## demography

Demographic data reveals that <mark><strong>perfectionism behaves as a widespread but unevenly distributed trait</strong><span> </span>across different populations</mark>. It is heavily influenced by factors such as age, generational shifts, gender socialization, and cultural environment. [link](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2021.688811/full)

The demographic breakdown of perfectionism shows the following key patterns:

### 📈 1. Generational Cohorts: The "Perfectionism Epidemic"

Large-scale, cross-temporal meta-analyses (such as the landmark studies by Dr. Thomas Curran and Dr. Andrew Hill) show that **younger generations are significantly more perfectionistic than their predecessors**. [link](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2026/05/young-adults-perfectionistic)

- **The Rise (1989–2026):** Looking at decades of data tracking college-aged cohorts across the US, UK, and Canada, perfectionism scores have skyrocketed. [link](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2026/05/young-adults-perfectionistic)
- **The Social Dimension:** Socially Prescribed Perfectionism (the feeling that society demands perfection) has seen the sharpest increase—surging by over 33% since the late 1980s.
- **Underlying Shift:** Psychologists attribute this generational surge to a hyper-competitive meritocracy, high-stakes testing, and the omnipresence of highly curated social media environments. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cb75brnf5iA\&t=115)

### ➡️ 2. Age and Development

Perfectionism tracks heavily with developmental life stages, hitting a crucial peak during identity formation: [link](https://byustudies.byu.edu/article/perfectionism-across-adolescence)

- **Adolescent Spike:** Longitudinal data shows that perfectionism takes a sharp upward trajectory between ages 12 and 18. As peer judgment and academic stakes increase, teens internalize a strict fear of failure. [link](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8727251/)
- **The Adult Trajectory:** While perfectionism can taper slightly as people find stable careers or experience life setbacks that force flexibility, it often mutates into occupational burnout or rigid parenting styles in middle age. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=cb75brnf5iA\&t=115)

### 🔎 3. Gender Differences

While both men and women experience perfectionism at similar absolute average rates, **females are significantly more likely to score in the highest percentiles for maladaptive (unhealthy) perfectionism**: [link](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9136022/)

- **Self-Critical Perfectionism:** Studies tracking young adults show that women report much higher levels of severe self-critical perfectionism and performance anxiety.
- **Socialization:** Psychologists link this gap to early childhood socialization, where girls are often disproportionately praised for being quiet, neat, compliant, and performing "perfectly," whereas boys are frequently given more latitude to be messy or take chaotic risks. [link](https://www.academia.edu/126206300/Perfectionism%5FIn%5FAdults%5FAssociations%5FWith%5FGender%5FAnd%5FSocioeconomic%5FStatus)

### 📊 4. Socioeconomic Status (SES)

Perfectionism does not affect all economic brackets equally: [link](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2021.688811/full)

- **High SES Correlation:** Research consistently shows a positive correlation between higher socioeconomic status (or a family's perceived economic well-being) and elevated levels of perfectionism.
- **Status Anxiety:** In affluent communities, there is often an intense "achievement culture" where children feel an crushing pressure to replicate or exceed their parents' financial and professional success. [link](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/public-health/articles/10.3389/fpubh.2021.688811/full)

### 💡 5. Cultural Differences

Cross-cultural psychology highlights that the internal motivation behind the trait varies significantly between Western and Eastern societies: [link](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0191886925003344)

- **Individualistic Cultures (e.g., US, Western Europe):** Perfectionism is primarily tied to personal ego, self-actualization, and individual status. Failure is viewed as a personal defect.
- **Collectivistic Cultures (e.g., East Asia):** Perfectionism is more frequently tied to social cohesion, familial duty, and maintaining group honor. In these cultures, high Self-Oriented Perfectionism is often driven by a deeply ingrained desire to avoid bringing shame upon the family unit. [link](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/386586035%5FPerfectionism%5FIn%5FAdults%5FAssociations%5FWith%5FGender%5FAnd%5FSocioeconomic%5FStatus)

## strategies

### Cognitive and Behavioral Strategies

Managing perfectionism requires retraining your thoughts, shifting your behaviors, and lowering the physical anxiety caused by high stress. The following clinical strategies from Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT) can help break perfectionist cycles:

#### 🧠 Cognitive Reframing (Changing Thought Patterns)

- **Challenge All-or-Nothing Thinking**: Force yourself to see the grey areas between "perfect success" and "total failure." A project that is 80% complete or a B+ grade is still a functional, positive outcome.
- **Track Your Inner Critic**: Write down your negative self-talk. Treat those harsh thoughts like an overly strict boss—ask yourself if you would ever say those exact words to a close friend.
- **Separate Worth from Work**: Remind yourself daily that your value as a human being is not tied to your productivity, income, or accomplishments.

#### ⚡ Behavioral Adjustments (Changing Actions)

- **Practice Behavioral Experiments**: Intentionally make a minor mistake to test your fears. Leave a text message unpunished by typos, send an email without proofreading it three times, or leave a few dishes in the sink overnight. Notice that nothing catastrophic happens.
- **Set "Good Enough" Limits**: Put strict time limits on tasks to prevent endless tweaking. Give yourself exactly 30 minutes to write a report, and force yourself to close the laptop when the timer stops.
- **Prioritize via "Value vs. Effort"**: Identify which tasks actually require high effort and which ones only need a basic attempt. Do not spend maximum energy on low-importance daily chores.

#### 🌊 Emotional and Physical Regulation

- **Build Tolerance for Discomfort**: When you leave a task imperfect, you will feel a wave of anxiety. Sit with that discomfort without rushing to fix the issue. Your nervous system will eventually calm down.
- **Celebrate the Process**: Force yourself to praise your effort rather than the final score. Focus on what you learned during a project instead of fixating only on the final evaluation.

***

### Professional Support Options

If perfectionism is causing severe procrastination, work burnout, or relationship issues, consider seeking outside help:

- **Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT)**: The gold-standard therapy framework for identifying, testing, and changing perfectionist beliefs.
- **Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)**: Focuses on accepting psychological discomfort and psychological flexibility rather than fighting to control every outcome.

## impact

The impact of perfectionism stretches across physical health, career development, relationships, and everyday emotional well-being. While it can drive short-term accomplishments, maladaptive perfectionism acts as a chronic tax on a person's life.

### 🧠 Mental Health Consequence

Perfectionism is a primary vulnerability factor for several clinical psychological conditions:

- **Depression and Anxiety**: The constant gap between actual performance and impossible ideals creates chronic feelings of hopelessness and a permanent state of hypervigilance.
- **Burnout Syndrome**: Because perfectionists struggle to rest or declare a task "finished," they run a high risk of total emotional and physical exhaustion.
- **Suicidal Ideation**: Clinical data shows that high Socially Prescribed Perfectionism (feeling that others demand perfection) is closely linked to deep loneliness and a perceived pressure to appear flawless.
- **Eating Disorders**: The drive for rigid control over life often shifts toward extreme, unyielding rules regarding body shape, weight, and food intake.

### 💼 Professional and Academic Career

While perfectionism is often mistaken for high ambition, it regularly undermines professional growth:

- **Paralyzing Procrastination**: The fear of making a mistake causes individuals to delay starting projects, which leads to missed deadlines and intense last-minute panic.
- **Loss of the "Big Picture"**: Getting trapped in minor details kills efficiency and prevents people from taking on high-level strategic leadership roles.
- **Extreme Risk Aversion**: Perfectionists rarely innovate because creative experimentation requires a willingness to fail, make a mess, and try again.

### 💔 Interpersonal Relationships

Holding unyielding standards strains connections with family, friends, and coworkers:

- **Other-Oriented Conflict**: Projecting high expectations onto a partner or child makes those loved ones feel constantly judged, criticized, and inadequate.
- **Emotional Distance**: Perfectionists often hide their struggles, vulnerabilities, and mistakes, which prevents them from building deep, authentic intimacy.
- **Fragile Teams**: In workplace settings, perfectionist managers struggle to delegate tasks, micromany their staff, and quickly crush team morale.

### 🫀 Physical Well-Being

The psychological stress of perfectionism directly impacts physical health through chronic activation of the nervous system:

- **Sleep Disturbances**: Late-night rumination over daily mistakes or tomorrow's workload often triggers severe insomnia.
- **Chronic Tension**: High-stress loops manifest physically as muscle tightness, tension headaches, and jaw clenching (bruxism).
- **Gastrointestinal Issues**: The gut-brain axis reacts heavily to prolonged anxiety, which frequently worsens symptoms of Irritable Bowel Syndrome (IBS).

## positive impact

While psychology research heavily focuses on the dangers of maladaptive perfectionism, <mark><strong>adaptive perfectionism</strong><span> </span>(often called "perfectionistic strivings") yields significant positive outcomes when managed healthily</mark>. When an individual can decouple their self-worth from their performance, setting high standards serves as a powerful engine for success and personal growth.

The primary positive impacts include:

### 🏆 1. Superior Performance and Skill Mastery

- **High-Level Execution**: Adaptive perfectionists naturally produce work of exceptional quality, depth, and precision, making them highly reliable in precision-dependent fields.
- **Deliberate Practice**: In fields like music, surgery, aviation, and athletics, the drive to refine micro-details facilitates the thousands of hours of focused practice required for true mastery.
- **Deep Domain Expertise**: The inclination to thoroughly investigate topics leads to a comprehensive, nuanced understanding of complex subjects.

### 🚀 2. Enhanced Motivation and Grit

- **Strong Goal Orientation**: Perfectionistic strivings provide clear, ambitious targets that give individuals a powerful sense of daily purpose and direction.
- **Intrinsic Satisfaction**: Reaching a difficult, self-imposed milestone provides profound feelings of personal fulfillment and self-actualization.
- **Resilience Through Engagement**: When motivated by a desire for excellence rather than a fear of failure, these individuals display high grit and stamina during difficult projects.

### 💼 3. Workplace and Academic Advantages

- **Natural Conscientiousness**: High conscientiousness is strongly linked to perfectionistic strivings, manifesting as excellent organization, punctuality, and thoroughness.
- **Proactive Problem Solving**: Rather than accepting subpar workarounds, adaptive perfectionists seek out highly efficient, optimized, and long-term systemic solutions.
- **Academic Achievement**: Meta-analytic studies consistently show a positive correlation between perfectionistic strivings and higher GPA or standardized test performance.

### 🌟 4. Personal Standards and Personal Growth

- **Continuous Self-Improvement**: These individuals maintain a lifelong commitment to learning, upgrading their skills, and refining their character.
- **Strong Ethical Accountability**: High internal standards frequently translate into a robust sense of personal responsibility, integrity, and reliability in commitments.

### The Fine Line: Adaptive vs. Maladaptive

The positive impacts of perfectionism only sustained if the trait remains **adaptive**.

| Feature                 | Adaptive (Positive)                       | Maladaptive (Negative)              |
| ----------------------- | ----------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Focus**               | Aiming for the **best possible** outcome. | Avoiding any **perceived mistake**. |
| **Reaction to Success** | Feeling pride, then resting.              | Feeling brief relief, then anxiety. |
| **Reaction to Failure** | "I made a mistake, let's learn."          | "I am a mistake, I am a failure."   |
