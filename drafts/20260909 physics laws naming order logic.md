## how are "the (number) law of (physic field)" named, why these numbers

The names and numbering of laws in physics follow a mix of historical discovery, logical hierarchy, and thermodynamic necessity. Rather than a single overarching rule, different fields of physics number their laws based on how those specific fields developed.

Here is how the numbering systems are named and why those specific numbers exist across the major fields of physics.

***

### 1. Thermodynamics: The Zeroeth through Third Laws

Thermodynamics features the most unique numbering system in physics because it includes a **"Zeroth" Law**.

- **The First Law (Conservation of Energy):** Formulated first in the 19th century by scientists like Rudolf Clausius and William Rankine. It states that energy cannot be created or destroyed.
- **The Second Law (Entropy):** Formulated around the same time, stating that the total entropy (disorder) of an isolated system always increases over time.
- **The Third Law (Absolute Zero):** Developed later (early 20th century) by Walther Nernst, stating that a system's entropy approaches a constant value as the temperature approaches absolute zero.
- **The Zeroth Law (Thermal Equilibrium):** Realized _after_ the First and Second laws were already widely accepted and named. Scientists (specifically Ralph H. Fowler in the 1930s) realized that to properly define temperature, they needed a foundational law: _If system A is in equilibrium with B, and B is in equilibrium with C, then A is in equilibrium with C._ Because it was more fundamental than the First Law, they couldn't call it the Third or Fourth Law, so they numbered it **Zero**.

***

### 2. Classical Mechanics: Newton’s Three Laws of Motion

Published by Sir Isaac Newton in his **1687** work, _Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica_, these laws are numbered sequentially to build a logical foundation for how objects move.

- **First Law (Inertia):** An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion stays in motion unless acted upon by an external force. This establishes the concept of a baseline "inertial frame."
- **Second Law (**

***

### 3. Planetary Motion: Kepler’s Three Laws

Johannes Kepler published his first two laws in **1609** and his third in **1619**. They are numbered based on the order in which he derived them from meticulous astronomical observations of Mars.

- **First Law (Law of Ellipses):** Planets move around the Sun in ellipses, with the Sun at one focus. (Defines the shape of the orbit).
- **Second Law (Law of Equal Areas):** A line segment joining a planet and the Sun sweeps out equal areas during equal intervals of time. (Defines how speed changes along that shape).
- **Third Law (Law of Harmonies):** The square of a planet's orbital period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis of its orbit. (A mathematical relationship comparing _different_ planets, discovered a decade later).

***

### 4. Electromagnetism: Maxwell’s Four Equations

While not strictly called "The First, Second, Third, and Fourth Laws," James Clerk Maxwell synthesized electromagnetism into **four fundamental equations** in the 1860s. They are numbered 1 through 4 in modern textbooks primarily for structural layout, though they represent distinct physical concepts:

- **First Equation (Gauss's Law for Electricity):** Describes how electric charges produce electric fields.
- **Second Equation (Gauss's Law for Magnetism):** States that there are no magnetic monopoles (magnetic field lines always form closed loops).
- **Third Equation (Faraday's Law of Induction):** Describes how a changing magnetic field creates an electric field.
- **Fourth Equation (Ampere's Law with Maxwell's Correction):** Describes how electric currents and changing electric fields create magnetic fields.

***

## why they are numbered this way

The numbering systems exist for three primary reasons: **logical building blocks, chronological discovery, and foundational corrections.**

Here is the exact breakdown of why each field settled on its specific numbers.

***

### 1. Thermodynamics: The Logic of "The Zeroth Law"

The numbers here reflect **logical priority** over chronological order.

- **The Problem:** The 1st and 2nd laws were named and accepted in the 1850s. Decades later, in the 1930s, scientists realized they had skipped a step. They were using thermometers to measure heat, but they hadn't actually defined _temperature_ mathematically.
- **The Solution:** To fix this, they formulated the Law of Thermal Equilibrium. Because this new law was the fundamental baseline required for the 1st and 2nd laws to even make sense, it couldn't be called the "3rd Law." It had to go before the 1st, so physicist Ralph Fowler called it the **Zeroth Law**.

### 2. Classical Mechanics: The Logic of Sequential Math

Newton numbered his laws of motion because they act as **sequential instructions** to solve a physics problem. One law physically unlocks the math for the next.

- **Step 1 (First Law):** First, you must establish if a system is stable. The First Law defines a baseline where forces equal zero (inertia).
- **Step 2 (Second Law):** Once you understand a stable system, you move to an unstable one. The Second Law tells you exactly what happens mathematically when a force is actually applied (F=ma).
- **Step 3 (Third Law):** Finally, you look at the bigger picture. The Third Law expands the math from a single object to how multiple objects push back against each other.

### 3. Planetary Motion: The Logic of Timeline

Kepler’s laws are numbered strictly by **chronological discovery** as he combed through decades of Mars data.

- **Laws 1 and 2** were discovered at the same time and published together in **1609**. They describe a _single_ planet's orbit (it's an ellipse, and it speeds up when closer to the Sun).
- **Law 3** required entirely new math to compare _multiple different_ planets to each other. It took Kepler another **10 years** of grueling calculations to find that pattern, publishing it later in 1619.

### 4. Electromagnetism: The Logic of Symmetry

Maxwell’s equations are numbered 1 through 4 to create a **perfect grid of physical scenarios**. They handle the relationships between fields (Electric vs. Magnetic) and sources (Static Charges vs. Moving Currents).

- **Equations 1 & 2** look at static, non-moving fields (Electric fields from static charges vs. Magnetic fields which have no individual "monopole" charges).
- **Equations 3 & 4** look at dynamic, moving fields (How a changing magnetic field creates electricity vs. How a changing electric field creates magnetism).

## who named each

Naming conventions across physics fields depend heavily on whether a single author formalized a text, or whether multiple 19th-century scientists were in a frantic race to publish.

Here is exactly who did the naming for each field of physics.

***

### 💡 1. Classical Mechanics: Sir Isaac Newton (Self-Named)

**Sir Isaac Newton** famously named his own laws. In his **1687** book, _Philosophiae Naturalis Principia Mathematica_, he explicitly laid them out in a dedicated introductory section titled **"Axiomata sive Leges Motus"** (Axioms or Laws of Motion). [link](https://pages.uoregon.edu/jschombe/ast122/lectures/lec03.html)

- He clearly labeled them as _Lex I_ (Law I), _Lex II_ (Law II), and _Lex III_ (Law III).
- He wrote them in Latin, and because it was his single, cohesive masterpiece, he assigned the numbers himself from the very beginning.

***

### 💡 2. Planetary Motion: Later Historians and Scientists

**Johannes Kepler** did _not_ explicitly name them the "First, Second, and Third Laws of Planetary Motion". [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List%5Fof%5Fscientific%5Flaws%5Fnamed%5Fafter%5Fpeople)

- Kepler published the first two laws in his book _Astronomia Nova_ (1609) and the third in _Harmonices Mundi_ (1619). He referred to them simply as key geometric theorems, insights, or "harmonies" about the solar system.
- It was **subsequent generations of physicists and textbook authors** in the late 17th and 18th centuries—most notably **Isaac Newton himself** in the _Principia_—who extracted Kepler's specific conclusions, grouped them together, and formally generalized them as _"Kepler's Three Laws"_ to credit his work.

***

### 💡 3. Thermodynamics: A Mix of Competitors and an Afterthought

Because thermodynamics was built pieces at a time by fierce rivals, the naming happened disjointedly: [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ocOHxPs1LQ0)

- **The First and Second Laws:** These were formally named by **William Thomson (Lord Kelvin)** and **Rudolf Clausius** around the **1850s–1860s**. Clausius and Thomson independently synthesized older concepts of heat energy into clear definitions, explicitly publishing them under the titles "The First Law" and "The Second Law". [link](https://www.britannica.com/science/laws-of-thermodynamics)
- **The Third Law:** This was named and popularized by subsequent physicists referring to the "Heat Theorem" developed by German chemist **Walther Nernst** between **1906 and 1912**. [link](https://www.britannica.com/science/laws-of-thermodynamics)
- **The Zeroth Law:** This specific name belongs entirely to British physicist **Ralph H. Fowler**. In the **1930s**, when reviewing a thermodynamics textbook written by Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar, Fowler pointed out that the concept of thermal equilibrium was missing a dedicated fundamental designation. Since the 1st and 2nd laws were already concrete in history, Fowler suggested calling it the **"Zeroth Law"**. [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeroth%5Flaw%5Fof%5Fthermodynamics)

***

### 💡 4. Electromagnetism: Albert Einstein and Oliver Heaviside

James Clerk Maxwell did not name the formulas "Maxwell's Four Equations". In 1865, he originally published a chaotic web of **20 separate mathematical equations**. [link](https://www.facebook.com/100094746404967/posts/maxwells-equations-%EF%B8%8F%EF%B8%8Fmaxwells-equations-formulated-by-james-clerk-maxwell-betwee/764843993350474/)

- **The Math:** In the 1880s, physicist **Oliver Heaviside** used modern vector calculus to condense Maxwell's 20 cluttered equations down into the clean **four** equations used today. For a long time, the scientific community actually called them the _"Hertz-Heaviside Equations"_.
- **The Name:** The name **"Maxwell's Equations"** was permanently popularized by **Albert Einstein** in a **1940** monograph titled _Considerations Concerning the Fundamentals of Theoretical Physics_. Einstein deeply revered Maxwell, viewing his field-theory unification as the most profound step forward in physics since Newton. Einstein's endorsement caused the global scientific community to drop Heaviside's name and give Maxwell full title ownership. [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maxwell%27s%5Fequations)
