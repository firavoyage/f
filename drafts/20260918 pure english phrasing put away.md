## does put away sth on somewhere mean where you put them or where they previously were

The phrase <mark><strong>"put something away" means placing an object in the storage location where it belongs or is normally kept</strong></mark>. [link](https://dictionary.cambridge.org/us/dictionary/english/put-away)

To answer your specific question directly: **it means the place where you are putting them now (their designated home), which happens to be where they previously were before you took them out to use them**. [link](https://www.collinsdictionary.com/dictionary/english/put-away)

Here is a breakdown of how the phrase works:

### 1. It refers to the final storage location

When you "put something away," you are moving it to its proper, long-term home so that it is out of sight and organized.

- **Example:** _"Please put your clothes away."_
- **Where do they go?** In the closet or dresser drawers (where you are putting them now). [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q3xQW8Tcdr8)

### 2. It implies returning something to its baseline state

Because it refers to the item's proper home, it inherently means you are returning it to **where it previously lived** before someone fetched it. [link](https://www.facebook.com/learn.english.with.maria.fernandez/videos/how-to-use-the-phrasal-verb-to-put-away-english-lesson/1097146470901527/)

- **Example:** If you take milk out of the fridge to pour a glass, and someone says, _"Put the milk away,"_ they mean return it to the fridge. [link](https://www.facebook.com/learn.english.with.maria.fernandez/videos/how-to-use-the-phrasal-verb-to-put-away-english-lesson/1097146470901527/)

### "Put away" vs. "Put down"

- If you **put something away**, you place it in its designated cabinet, drawer, box, or shelf.
- If you just **put something down**, you are dropping it on the nearest flat surface (like a table or counter), which is temporary and might _not_ be where it belongs. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=q3xQW8Tcdr8)

## if you say put sth on somewhere away what does somewhere mean

If you say **"put \[something] on \[somewhere] away,"** the phrasing is slightly mixed up, but it usually means <mark><strong>the temporary spot where the item currently is, and you need to move it into its proper storage.</strong></mark>

However, because English speakers don't usually say "put something _on somewhere_ away," the exact meaning depends on how the sentence is put together. Here are the two most likely scenarios:

### 1. You mean: "Put away the things that are on \[somewhere]."

If someone says, _"Put the things on the table away,"_ **somewhere** means the **current, messy location** where the items are sitting right now. You are being asked to take them _off_ that surface and move them to their proper storage homes.

### 2. You mean: "Put that away on \[somewhere]."

If someone says, _"Put that away on the top shelf,"_ **somewhere** means the **final storage destination**. You are being given specific instructions on exactly which shelf or cabinet counts as "away" for that item.

### Quick Summary

- **"Put away the books on the floor"** → _Somewhere_ is the **current messy spot** (the floor).
- **"Put the books away on the shelf"** → _Somewhere_ is the **final storage spot** (the shelf).
