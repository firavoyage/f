### Real-World Examples

- **A faulty scale**: If you weigh a 5 kg weight and get 7.1 kg, 7.0 kg, and 7.1 kg, the scale is **precise** (consistent) but **not accurate** (incorrect).
- **A loose target**: If an archer hits all around the bullseye but the arrows are widely scattered, the average position is **accurate** (on target) but **not precise** (not grouped).
- **The ideal measurement**: Hitting the exact center of the bullseye with every single arrow represents being both **accurate and precise**.

# compare accuracy and precision

### The Core Difference

**Accuracy** is how close a measurement is to the true or target value.\
**Precision** is how close a series of measurements are to each other, regardless of the target.

### Visual Analogy (The Dartboard)

- **Accurate and Precise**: Darts are tightly clustered directly in the bulls-eye.
- **Precise but Inaccurate**: Darts are tightly clustered together, but far away from the bulls-eye.
- **Accurate but Imprecise**: Darts are scattered all around the bulls-eye, but their average center is the bulls-eye.
- **Inaccurate and Imprecise**: Darts are scattered randomly across the entire board.

### Key Differences

| Feature         | Accuracy                                | Precision                                 |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------- |
| **Definition**  | Closeness to the true value.            | Closeness of multiple measurements.       |
| **Focus**       | Correctness of the result.              | Repeatability and consistency of results. |
| **Measurement** | Requires comparing to a known standard. | Requires comparing multiple trials.       |
| **Error Type**  | Affected by systematic error (bias).    | Affected by random error (noise).         |

### Real-World Example

Imagine a scale weighing a standard **10-pound** weight:

- The scale reads 10.0 lbs, 10.0 lbs, and 10.0 lbs. It is **accurate and precise**.
- The scale reads 13.1 lbs, 13.1 lbs, and 13.2 lbs. It is **precise but inaccurate**.
- The scale reads 8.0 lbs, 12.0 lbs, and 10.0 lbs. It is **accurate (on average) but imprecise**.

### The Four Measurement Scenarios

- **High Accuracy, High Precision**: Measurements are tightly grouped together and hit the exact center of the true target value.
- **Low Accuracy, High Precision**: Measurements are tightly grouped together but are far away from the true target value.
- **High Accuracy, Low Precision**: Measurements are scattered widely but their average closely matches the true target value.
- **Low Accuracy, Low Precision**: Measurements are scattered widely across the board and miss the true target value completely.
