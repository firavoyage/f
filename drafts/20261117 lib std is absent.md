# what are the alternative terms for nullish

In programming, particularly in JavaScript and TypeScript, **nullish** specifically refers to values that are either `null` or `undefined`. While "nullish" is the standard technical term used in official documentation like [MDN Web Docs](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Operators/Nullish%5Fcoalescing), developers and different programming languages use several alternative terms depending on the context. [](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Reference/Operators/Nullish%5Fcoalescing)

### Programming & Computer Science Alternatives

* **Nullable.** Commonly used in languages like C#, Kotlin, and Swift to describe a variable or type that can hold a `null` value.
* **Nil or Nil-like.** Used in languages such as Ruby, Go, Lua, and Objective-C to represent the absence of a value.
* **Undefined or Unset.** Used when referring to variables that have been declared without an assigned value.
* **Empty or Void.** Often used in database contexts or older languages to indicate no data is present, though "empty" can sometimes include empty strings (`""`) or arrays.
* **None or Nothing.** Standard terminology in languages like Python (`None`), Haskell (`Nothing`), and Rust (`None` within an `Option` type).
* **Absent or Missing.** Frequently used in plain-English documentation and type-checking discussions to describe values that have not been provided. [](https://javascript.plainenglish.io/understanding-nullish-non-nullish-and-asserting-non-nullish-values-in-typescript-5a753fa0254d)

### Related Terms (With Different Meanings)

* **Falsy.** Often confused with nullish, a falsy value is any value that evaluates to `false` in a boolean context. This includes `null` and `undefined`, but also encompasses `0`, `""` (empty string), `NaN`, and `false` itself.
* **Blank.** Used in frameworks like Ruby on Rails to describe values that are `nil`, `false`, empty, or consist only of whitespace. [](https://dev.to/rutuja%5Fweb%5Fdev/understanding-the-nullish-coalescing-operator-vs-logical-or-in-javascript-5263)

### General English Synonyms

If you are using "nullish" outside of a coding context to mean "having no value or effect," standard alternatives include:

* **Null or Void.** Having no legal or binding force.
* **Negligible or Insignificant.** Too small or unimportant to be worth considering.
* **Nonexistent or Blank.** Completely absent or lacking content. [](https://www.merriam-webster.com/thesaurus/nullify)

# compare absent and missing

**Absent** means not present at a specific place or event, while **missing** means lost, unaccounted for, or unable to be found. Both describe something or someone not being there, but they differ in whether the location or reason is known. [](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/absent)

### Key Differences

| Feature            | Absent                                                   | Missing                                                    |
| ------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Core Meaning**   | Simply not present at a given time or place.             | Lost, disappeared, or cannot be located.                   |
| **Whereabouts**    | Often known, planned, or easily explained.               | Unknown or unaccounted for.                                |
| **Expectation**    | Used for scheduled events like class, work, or meetings. | Used when something belongs in a set or a person vanishes. |
| **Emotional Tone** | Neutral, routine, or administrative.                     | Urgent, concerning, or incomplete.                         |

### When to Use Each

* **Use "absent" for attendance and presence.** It describes a person who did not show up to a routine obligation like school or work, often for a known reason such as illness or vacation. You can also use it for abstract traits, such as saying empathy is absent from a decision. [](https://ell.stackexchange.com/questions/13473/absent-vs-missing)
* **Use "missing" for lost people or incomplete items.** For people, it implies their whereabouts are unknown and they may be in danger, such as in a missing person report. For objects or data, it means a necessary piece is gone, like a missing puzzle piece or a missing file. [](https://researchportal.port.ac.uk/en/publications/absent-an-exploration-of-common-police-procedures-for-safeguardin/)
* **Legal and police distinctions.** Law enforcement and courts treat these terms differently. For example, UK police definitions classify someone as **absent** if they are not where they are expected to be without apparent risk, whereas **missing** applies when their whereabouts are unknown and they face potential harm. [](https://www.malnassarlawyer.com/en/the-absent-and-the-missing)

### Examples in Context

* **Absent.** "Sarah is **absent** from class today because she has a dentist appointment."
* **Missing.** "Sarah is **missing**; nobody has seen or heard from her since yesterday morning."
* **Objects.** "Three pages are **missing** from this book," versus "Humor is completely **absent** from this book." [](https://ell.stackexchange.com/questions/13473/absent-vs-missing)
