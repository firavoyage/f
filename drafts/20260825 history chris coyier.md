## background

Chris Coyier's background combines **classical design training, self-taught programming, and a foundational career in the Midwest** before he became a prominent figure in the web development industry.

### Educational Pivot

- **Ceramics & Graphic Design**: Coyier attended the University of Wisconsin-Whitewater. He graduated with a Bachelor of Arts degree, focusing heavily on ceramics and traditional graphic design rather than computer science.
- **Self-Taught Coding**: Realizing the potential of the web, he taught himself HTML, CSS, PHP, and JavaScript immediately after college to translate his visual design skills into digital products.

### Early Professional Career

- **Print to Web**: His earliest professional roles in the mid-2000s were in printing shops and small design agencies in Madison, Wisconsin. He handled pre-press work, layout design, and eventually basic website building.
- **Agency Experience**: He later moved to corporate and agency environments, working as a front-end developer for companies like firewheel and eventually standardizing web design practices at various startups.

### Founding of CSS-Tricks (2007)

- **The Launch**: In 2007, Coyier launched CSS-Tricks as a personal blog to document his own learning journey, code snippets, and solutions to common browser bugs.
- **The Growth**: The site quickly evolved from a personal diary into the internet's definitive educational repository for modern CSS techniques, establishing him as an authority in front-end development long before he co-founded CodePen.

## opinions

Chris Coyier has long been an influential voice in the web ecosystem, known for his **pragmatic, web-standards-focused opinions** on technology, career longevity, and creative work. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uJrh9GHrC38)

### 🤖 The Impact of AI on Coding & Careers

- **The Reality of AI Code**: Coyier openly acknowledges that modern AI coding tools like Cursor, Claude, and ChatGPT are highly effective, stating, "The worst fact about these tools is that they work."
- **The Future of Human Value**: Despite experiencing genuine productivity boosts from using AI, he believes the role of a developer must shift. Because AI writes syntax efficiently, human value lies in high-level architecture: **planning, communication, prioritization, accountability, and dreaming**. [link](https://chriscoyier.net/2025/07/23/impact-of-ai-on-tech-content-creators/)
- **Content Creation Ethics**: He openly critiques the "slimy" data-scraping practices of major AI companies, yet argues that creators shouldn't simply "take their ball and go home," choosing instead to lean into the undeniable user benefits of AI workflows. [link](https://chriscoyier.net/2025/07/23/impact-of-ai-on-tech-content-creators/)

### 🎨 Web Capabilities & Frameworks

- **The Power of Vanilla Web**: A massive advocate for the modern web platform, Coyier frequently argues that **HTML and CSS can now natively replace heavy JavaScript libraries**. He regularly showcases how to build tooltips, complex view transitions, and layout resets using pure browser standards. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uJrh9GHrC38)
- **Web vs. Native Mobile**: He holds a strong stance that mobile web app capabilities have matured to a level where they can perform universally and effectively match native mobile apps. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uJrh9GHrC38)
- **The "All-Powerful" Frontend Developer**: He popularised the idea that front-end engineers are incredibly powerful because modern backend-as-a-service APIs allow them to build complete, robust, full-stack digital products entirely on their own.

### ✍️ Writing with Conviction

- **Against Softening Ideas**: On his personal blog, Coyier expressed a strong dislike for filler phrases like _"in my opinion," "I think,"_ or _"some might disagree."_ He views these phrases as shields that make public writing fragile and weak. He believes that if you are writing something, it is obviously your opinion, and you should **state your thoughts with conviction** rather than bowing to internet commenters.
