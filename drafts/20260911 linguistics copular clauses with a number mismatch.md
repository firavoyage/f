## what are they called in linguistics

In linguistics, these sentences are called <mark><strong>copular clauses</strong></mark> (or copular constructions), and the specific relationship between the plural and singular noun phrases is known as **inverse agreement** or **unbalanced agreement**.

Here is how linguists break down the components and the different types of these clauses:

### 1. The Core Components

Linguists use specific terminology for the parts of this sentence structure:

- **The Copula:** The linking verb ("be", "are", "is").
- **The Subject:** The initial plural noun phrase.
- **The Predicate Nominal:** The singular noun phrase that follows the verb and describes or renames the subject.

### 2. The Type of Copular Clause

Depending on the exact meaning of your sentence, linguists categorize them into two main types:

- **Predicative Copular Clauses:** These assign a property or category to the subject.
  - _Example:_ "Cats are a great comfort." \[5]
  - _Linguistic view:_ "Cats" belong to the broader class or property of being "a great comfort." \[5]
- **Equative (or Identificational) Copular Clauses:** These state that the subject and the predicate nominal are the exact same entity.
  - _Example:_ "The only thing I need are new shoes." (Often flipped as: "New shoes are the only thing I need.") \[6]
  - _Linguistic view:_ Both sides of the verb refer to the identical entity or identity.

### 3. The Agreement Phenomenon

When the subject is plural and the predicate is singular, the verb has to "choose" which one to agree with. This triggers specific linguistic concepts:

- **Subject-Verb Agreement:** In English, standard syntax dictates that the verb must agree with the _syntactic subject_ (the plural noun), regardless of what comes after the verb.
- **Copular Agreement Mismatch:** This is the term for when the subject and the predicate nominal do not match in number (one is plural, one is singular), forcing a structural mismatch that the language's grammar must resolve.

## what are "plural noun phrase are singular noun phrase" sentences called

In linguistics, sentences with the structure _"plural noun phrase + are + singular noun phrase"_ are broadly classified as <mark><strong>copular clauses with a number mismatch</strong></mark>.

Depending on the specific subfield of linguistics (syntax vs. semantics), they are described using a few highly precise terms:

### 1. By Sentence Type: Specificational Copular Clauses

Linguists categorize copular sentences (sentences using "to be") based on how they deliver information. While a standard sentence like _"Cats are animals"_ is a **predicational** clause (assigning a category to a subject), sentences that introduce a list, value, or definition are called **specificational copular clauses**. - **Example:** _"The main obstacles are a lack of funding."_

### 2. By Agreement Phenomenon: Unbalanced or Asymmetric Agreement

When the subject and the predicate nominal disagree in grammatical number (one is plural, one is singular), it triggers what linguists call a **number agreement mismatch**. [link](https://search.proquest.com/openview/191e7dae920ef47628adceabe3688cd7/1?pq-origsite=gscholar\&cbl=5586516)

Linguists study the direction of these sentences to see which phrase holds the most structural "weight":

- **Canonical Order:** _Plural Subject + Are + Singular Predicate_ (e.g., _"Computers are a vital tool."_)
- **Inverse Copular Clause:** When you flip it so that the singular phrase comes first, the verb changes to match it (e.g., _"A vital tool is computers."_).

# copular clauses

A **copular clause** is a grammatical construction where the subject is joined to a description or an identity (the subject complement) by a [copula or linking verb](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copula%5F%28linguistics%29). Instead of expressing a dynamic action, a copular clause functions like an algebraic equal sign (=), establishing a relationship, state of being, or definition. [link](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Copula%5F%28linguistics%29)

In English, the most common copular verb is **to be** (in forms like _is, am, are, was, were_). Other common linking verbs include _seem, appear, become, feel, sound, taste,_ and _smell_. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bdS8mcTxiwk)

***

### The Three Main Types of Copular Clauses

Linguists generally classify copular clauses into three primary semantic categories based on how the subject and complement interact: [link](https://benjamins.com/catalog/la.85)

| Type                     | Definition                                                                                                                                              | Example                                                           |
| ------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Predicational**        | Assigns a property, characteristic, or quality to the subject. The subject is a specific entity, and the complement is an adjective or indefinite noun. | _“John is honest.”_ _“The sky is blue.”_                          |
| **Specificational**      | Pinpoints or identifies _who_ or _what_ fills a specific role or description. It essentially "specifies" a value.                                       | _“The winner is Sarah.”_ _“The cause of the riot was a picture.”_ |
| **Equative (Identical)** | Asserts that two distinct referential expressions point to the exact same entity. Both sides are typically definite names or titles.                    | _“Cicero is Tully.”_ _“She is the Mayor of Cambridge.”_           |

***

### Key Linguistic Characteristics

- **Adjectives over Adverbs:** Because copular verbs describe states rather than actions, they are followed by adjectives (subject complements) instead of adverbs. For example, you say _"The soup smells delicious"_ (adjective), not _"The soup smells deliciously"_ (adverb). [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bdS8mcTxiwk)
- **Inversion Properties:** Predicational and specificational clauses behave differently when flipped. For instance, a predicational clause like _"John is a philosopher"_ cannot easily be inverted into `*`_"A philosopher is John"_. However, specificational clauses allow inversion because the predicate can sometimes occupy the subject position. [link](https://www.ic.nanzan-u.ac.jp/LINGUISTICS/publication/pdf/NL3-2-arimoto.pdf)
- **Lack of Semantic Roles:** Unlike action verbs (like _kick_ or _write_), copular verbs do not assign traditional semantic agent/patient roles to their arguments. Their primary purpose is to act as a structural bridge. [link](https://dlc.hypotheses.org/3865)

# copular clauses with a number mismatch

## copular clauses with a number mismatch

In English syntax, **copular clauses with a number mismatch** <mark>occur when the subject noun phrase (DP1) and the predicative complement / predicate nominal (DP2) differ in grammatical number</mark>. This mismatch creates a conflict for verb agreement: should the copular verb (_be_) agree with the singular or the plural element? [link](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7026494/)

How this is resolved depends heavily on the **clause type** and **structural constraints**.

***

### 1. Clause Type Matters: Predicational vs. Specificational

The behavior of the verb changes depending on the semantic function of the copular clause: [link](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/344039870%5FCopular%5FClauses%5FSpecification%5Fpredication%5Fand%5Fequation)

- **Predicational Clauses:** The subject is a referential entity and the complement describes a property of it.
  - _Example:_ "The only thing I see **is** \[singular] swirling buzzards \[plural]."
  - In English, the verb almost consistently matches the grammatical number of the leftmost subject (DP1), even if it sounds slightly unnatural. [link](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2019.02994/full)
- **Specificational Clauses:** The clause essentially provides a value to a variable. The subject sets up the category and the complement specifies what it is.
  - _Example:_ "The main problem **is / are** the missing documents."
  - These are highly prone to agreement variation because speakers balance syntactic rules against conceptual information from the whole sentence. [link](https://journals.linguisticsociety.org/proceedings/index.php/ExtendedAbs/article/view/575)

### 2. Agreement Cross-Linguistically

Languages resolve number mismatches in copular constructions using different default strategies: [link](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7026494/)

| Language      | Agreement Strategy                     | Example Pattern                                                                 |
| ------------- | -------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **English**   | **DP1 Agreement** (First noun phrase)  | "The problem **is** \[_singular_] the results \[_plural_]."                     |
| **Italian**   | **DP2 Agreement** (Second noun phrase) | _Il problema **sono** \[plural] i risultati \[plural]._                         |
| **Icelandic** | **Number-only DP2 Agreement**          | Verb agrees with the post-copular phrase in number, but not necessarily person. |

### 3. Notional vs. Proximity Concord

When native English speakers deviate from strict DP1 agreement, two linguistic factors are usually at play:

1. **Notional Concord:** Agreement based on the _meaning_ rather than the strict grammatical form. For example, a collective noun like "committee" is grammatically singular but notionally plural, facilitating a mismatch like _"The committee are actors"_. [link](https://nagaselv.weebly.com/uploads/9/7/9/1/97911292/naga%5Fdissertation.pdf)
2. **Proximity Concord:** The tendency for the verb to agree with whichever noun phrase is closest to it in linear order. In speech, it is very common to hear _"The only option left **were** the double beds"_ because the plural noun immediately follows the verb.

## why is it called dp1/dp2

The terms **DP1** and **DP2** stand for **Determiner Phrase 1** and **Determiner Phrase 2**.

In modern **generative grammar (syntax)**, linguists use "DP" instead of the traditional term "NP" (Noun Phrase) to describe the structural makeup of a noun and its modifiers. The numbers **1** and **2** simply refer to the linear order in which they appear in the clause: **DP1** is the first phrase (the subject) and **DP2** is the second phrase (the complement).

***

### Why "Determiner Phrase" (DP) and not "Noun Phrase" (NP)?

Under traditional grammar, a phrase like _"the results"_ is called a Noun Phrase because the noun (_results_) is the most important word.

However, modern syntactic theory argues that the **determiner** (words like _the, a, this, those, my_) is actually the "head" or the boss of the phrase. The determiner controls the grammatical properties of the whole unit, such as its definiteness and how it interacts with the verb.

Therefore, syntacticians visualize the structure like a hierarchy:

- **DP** (Determiner Phrase)\
  →\
  headed by _"the"_
  - **NP** (Noun Phrase)\
    →\
    headed by _"results"_

### The Copular Anatomy

When analyzing a copular clause (a sentence with a linking verb like _be_), linguists map it out schematically to track exactly how elements move and trigger agreement:

\[DP1]+CopulaVerb+\[DP2]

- **DP1 (The Subject):**\
  TheproblemDP1\
  is the results.
- **DP2 (The Complement):** The problem is\
  theresultsDP2\
  .

Using **DP1** and **DP2** gives linguists a neutral, algebraic way to talk about the two sides of the equation. This notation makes it easy to discuss abstract syntactic movements—such as how languages decide whether the verb should look left to **DP1** or right to **DP2** to get its singular or plural marking.

## compare dp1 and dp2 agreement

The choice between **DP1 agreement** and **DP2 agreement** determines whether a copular verb looks left to the subject or right to the complement to determine its grammatical number.

Here is a direct comparison of how these two structural strategies operate:

| Feature                          | DP1 Agreement (Subject)                                                                             | DP2 Agreement (Complement)                                                                                              |
