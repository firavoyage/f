## Question 1: Gibbs free energy and spontaneity

Correct answer: D — The system is at equilibrium.

The Gibbs free energy change, \\(\Delta G\\), determines whether a reaction is spontaneous at constant temperature and pressure.

- \\(\Delta G<0\\): The forward reaction is spontaneous.
- \\(\Delta G>0\\): The forward reaction is non-spontaneous.
- \\(\Delta G=0\\): The system is at equilibrium.

Therefore, when the Gibbs free energy change becomes zero, the reaction is at equilibrium.

Key concept: \\(\boxed{\Delta G=0 \Rightarrow \text{equilibrium}}\\)

## Question 2: Change in internal energy

Correct answer: D — \\(-30\text{ kJ}\\)

Given:

- Work done by the system on the surroundings: \\(20\text{ kJ}\\)
- Heat released by the system to the surroundings: \\(10\text{ kJ}\\)

The first law of thermodynamics is

\\[ \Delta U=q+w \\]

where:

- \\(q\\) is heat absorbed by the system.
- \\(w\\) is work done on the system.

Because the system releases heat,

\\[ q=-10\text{ kJ} \\]

Because the system does work on the surroundings,

\\[ w=-20\text{ kJ} \\]

Substituting,

\\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta U&=(-10)+(-20)\\\ &=-30\text{ kJ} \end{aligned} \\]

Therefore,

\\[ \boxed{\Delta U=-30\text{ kJ}} \\]

Key concept: Heat released and work done by the system both decrease its internal energy.

## Question 3: Reactions with positive standard entropy change

The question asks which reaction has \\(\Delta_r S_m^\circ>0\\).

The options are:

- A. \\(2H_2(g)+O_2(g)\rightarrow2H_2O(g)\\)
- B. \\(N_2(g)+3H_2(g)\rightarrow2NH_3(g)\\)
- C. \\(NH_4Cl(s)\rightarrow NH_3(g)+HCl(g)\\)
- D. \\(C(s)+O_2(g)\rightarrow CO_2(g)\\)

### Explanation

Entropy measures the dispersal of energy and the number of accessible microscopic arrangements.

- A: Three moles of gaseous reactants become two moles of gaseous products. Entropy generally decreases.
- B: Four moles of gaseous reactants become two moles of gaseous products. Entropy decreases.
- C: A solid decomposes into two gases. Entropy increases substantially.
- D: One mole of gas becomes one mole of gas, with solid carbon also consumed. The entropy change is small and positive when standard molar entropy data are used.

For option C,

\\[ NH_4Cl(s)\rightarrow NH_3(g)+HCl(g) \\]

The formation of two gases from a solid produces a substantial increase in entropy.

Likely intended answer: C.

Important scientific note: Using actual standard molar entropy values, option D also has a slightly positive entropy change, approximately \\(+2.9\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\\). Thus, if the question allows only one answer, it is technically ambiguous. Option C is the clearly positive, conventionally intended answer.

## Question 4: Temperature and spontaneity

Correct answer: D — At high temperatures, the forward reaction is non-spontaneous; at low temperatures, it is spontaneous.

The reaction is

\\[ Mg(s)+Cl_2(g)\rightarrow MgCl_2(s) \\]

Given:

\\[ \Delta_r H_m^\circ=-642\text{ kJ mol}^{-1} \\]

The reaction is exothermic because its enthalpy change is negative.

The reactants include a gas, while the product is a solid. The entropy change is therefore negative:

\\[ \Delta S<0 \\]

The Gibbs free energy equation is

\\[ \Delta G=\Delta H-T\Delta S \\]

Since both \\(\Delta H\\) and \\(\Delta S\\) are negative:

- At low temperatures: The negative enthalpy term dominates, so \\(\Delta G<0\\). The forward reaction is spontaneous.
- At high temperatures: The term \\(-T\Delta S\\) becomes increasingly positive, so \\(\Delta G\\) can become positive. The forward reaction is non-spontaneous.

Therefore,

\\[ \boxed{\text{Low }T:\text{ spontaneous; high }T:\text{ non-spontaneous}} \\]

Key concept: A reaction with \\(\Delta H<0\\) and \\(\Delta S<0\\) tends to be spontaneous at low temperatures.

## Question 5: Calculate the equilibrium constant \\(K^\circ\\) at 523 K

The reaction is

\\[ CO(g)+3H_2(g)\rightleftharpoons CH_4(g)+H_2O(g) \\]

The given data are:

| Substance | \\(\Delta_f H_m^\circ\\) (kJ mol\\(^{-1}\\)) | \\(S_m^\circ\\) (J mol\\(^{-1}\\) K\\(^{-1}\\)) |
| --------- | -------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- |
| CO(g)     | −110.525                                     | 197.674                                         |
| H₂(g)     | 0                                            | 130.684                                         |
| CH₄(g)    | −74.81                                       | 186.264                                         |
| H₂O(g)    | −241.818                                     | 88.825                                          |

We need to calculate the standard equilibrium constant at \\(T=523\text{ K}\\).

### Step 1: Calculate the standard enthalpy change

Use the formula

\\[ \Delta_r H^\circ =\sum \Delta_f H^\circ\_{\text{products}} -\sum \Delta_f H^\circ\_{\text{reactants}} \\]

Substituting the values,

\\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta_r H^\circ &=[-74.81+(-241.818)]\\\ &\quad-[-110.525+3(0)]\\\ &=-316.628+110.525\\\ &=\boxed{-206.103\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}} \end{aligned} \\]

### Step 2: Calculate the standard entropy change

Use

\\[ \Delta_r S^\circ =\sum S^\circ\_{\text{products}} -\sum S^\circ\_{\text{reactants}} \\]

Substituting,

\\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta_r S^\circ &=[186.264+88.825]\\\ &\quad-[197.674+3(130.684)]\\\ &=275.089-589.726\\\ &=\boxed{-314.637\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}} \end{aligned} \\]

Convert entropy to kJ units to match the enthalpy:

\\[ \Delta_r S^\circ=-0.314637 \text{ kJ mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1} \\]

### Step 3: Calculate the Gibbs free energy change at 523 K

Use

\\[ \Delta_r G^\circ=\Delta_r H^\circ-T\Delta_r S^\circ \\]

Substitute \\(T=523\text{ K}\\):

\\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta_r G^\circ &=-206.103-523(-0.314637)\\\ &=-206.103+164.656\\\ &=\boxed{-41.45\text{ kJ mol}^{-1}} \end{aligned} \\]

The slight difference from rounding intermediate values is negligible.

### Step 4: Calculate the equilibrium constant

The relationship between Gibbs free energy and the standard equilibrium constant is

\\[ \Delta_r G^\circ=-RT\ln K^\circ \\]

Rearrange:

\\[ \ln K^\circ=-\frac{\Delta_r G^\circ}{RT} \\]

Use:

- \\(R=8.314\text{ J mol}^{-1}\text{K}^{-1}\\)
- \\(T=523\text{ K}\\)
- \\(\Delta_r G^\circ\approx-41{,}450\text{ J mol}^{-1}\\)

Then,

\\[ \begin{aligned} \ln K^\circ &=\frac{41{,}450}{8.314(523)}\\\ &\approx9.53 \end{aligned} \\]

Taking the exponential,

\\[ K^\circ=e^{9.53} \\]

\\[ \boxed{K^\circ\approx1.4\times10^4} \\]

Final answer for Question 5: The standard equilibrium constant at 523 K is approximately \\(14{,}000\\).

Because \\(K^\circ\gg1\\), the equilibrium strongly favors the products under standard-state conventions.
