compare factor multiplier and coefficient

While **factors**, **multipliers**, and **coefficients** all involve multiplication, they serve different purposes in mathematics, science, and economics.

**Factors** are the building blocks that are multiplied together to create a product. A **coefficient** is a specific type of factor—usually a constant number placed in front of a variable. A **multiplier** is a scale factor that amplifies or scales a starting value, often used to show cause-and-effect changes over time.

### Summary of Differences

Think of it as a hierarchy of specificity. Every coefficient is technically a factor, but not every factor is a coefficient. Meanwhile, a multiplier is a specialized tool used to calculate the broad, cascading ripple effects of multiplying a starting number.
