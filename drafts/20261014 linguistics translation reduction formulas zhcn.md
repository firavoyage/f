# what do ppl criticize on the translation on zhcn textbook of "诱导公式"

The criticism surrounding the translation of **诱导公式** (Yòudǎo Gōngshì) in Simplified Chinese (zh-cn) math textbooks centers on <mark>a widespread<span> </span><strong>mistranslation in the English annotations</strong><span> </span>and the<span> </span><strong>confusing historical origin</strong><span> </span>of the Chinese term itself</mark>.

Math educators, students, and bilingual learners frequently criticize two major flaws: [link](https://www.zhihu.com/question/27192923/answer/2035587808)

### 1. The "Induction" vs. "Reduction" Blunder

In many Chinese textbooks (such as the widely used PEP / 人教版 Mathematics), the English translation provided for _诱导公式_ is **"induction formula"**. This is heavily criticized as a mathematical and linguistic error: [link](https://www.zhihu.com/question/27192923/answer/2035587808)

- **What it should be:** Globally, these formulas are called **reduction formulas** because their purpose is to _reduce_ or simplify trigonometric functions of large or negative angles into basic acute angles. [link](https://user.guancha.cn/main/content?id=1503057\&s=fwzwyzzwzbt)
- **Why "induction" is wrong:** In mathematics, "induction" strictly refers to _mathematical induction_ (数学归纳法), a completely unrelated proof technique. Using "induction formula" for trigonometric reduction confuses students trying to look up international math resources, as searching for "induction formula" online returns results about electromagnetism or mathematical proofs rather than trigonometry. [link](https://www.zhihu.com/question/27192923/answer/2035587808)

### 2. The Awkward Historical Origin of "诱导"

The criticism also extends to the Chinese term itself, which does not intuitively reflect what the formulas actually do. [link](https://user.guancha.cn/main/content?id=1503057\&s=fwzwyzzwzbt)

- **The Soviet Influence:** During the 1950s, China’s mathematics curriculum heavily borrowed from Soviet Union textbooks. The Russian term for these identities is _Формулы приведения_, which translates to "conversion formulas" or "bringing-over formulas". [link](https://user.guancha.cn/main/content?id=1503057\&s=fwzwyzzwzbt)
- **Lost in Translation:** The choice to translate this into "诱导" (which means to guide, induce, or lure) is considered an awkward linguistic legacy. Critics argue that terms like _化简公式_ (simplification formulas) or _约化公式_ (reduction formulas) would have been far more intuitive for Chinese students. [link](https://www.zhihu.com/question/27192923/answer/2035587808)

### International Equivalents

In western math curricula (such as those on Khan Academy), these formulas are rarely grouped under a single umbrella term like they are in China. Instead, they are broken down by their exact geometric properties: [link](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2070924059643320295)

- **Cofunction identities:** For 90° ± α (shifting between sine and cosine).
- **Symmetry and periodicity identities:** For 180° ± α or 360° ± α. [link](https://zhuanlan.zhihu.com/p/2070924059643320295)

## why do they translate this way

The confusing "double translation" happened because of **two distinct historical accidents**: one during the 1950s that created the Chinese term "诱导", and another much later when textbook editors blindly translated it into English. [link](https://www.zhihu.com/question/568998026)

***

### Phase 1: How it became "诱导" in Chinese (The 1950s)

In the 1950s, China radically overhauled its education system to align with the Soviet Union. Soviet math textbooks were translated into Chinese rapidly and en masse. [link](https://www.zhihu.com/question/568998026)

1. **The Russian Term:** In Russian, these formulas are called **формулы приведения** (_formuly privedeniya_). [link](https://www.zhihu.com/question/568998026)
2. **The Linguistic Trap:** The Russian root verb _приводить_ means "to bring over," "to reduce," or "to lead/guide".

- In a mathematical context, it meant "to reduce/convert" a large angle down to a sharp acute angle.
- However, _приводить_ also translates colloquially to **"to induce, to guide, or to lure."** [link](https://www.zhihu.com/question/65830706)

3. **The Translation Choice:** In 1953, translator Li Rongdong published a translation of a Soviet trigonometry textbook. Instead of selecting the mathematical meaning ("reduction/simplification"), he localized it into the word **诱导 (Yòudǎo - to induce/guide)**.
4. **The Post-Hoc Justification:** Because textbooks never explained _why_ it was called "诱导", generations of Chinese teachers invented an explanation to make sense of it. They taught students that the formulas **"induce or guide"** a massive, complex angle back into a familiar, friendly acute angle. The word stuck and became a locked-in standard. [link](https://www.zhihu.com/question/65830706)

***

### Phase 2: How it became "Induction" in English (The Modern Era)

Decades later, as Chinese textbooks began adding English translations to math terms to align with international education standards, editors faced a problem: **"How do we translate _诱导公式_ back into English?"** [link](https://www.zhihu.com/question/568998026)

Rather than researching what western mathematics actually called them ([reduction formulas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List%5Fof%5Ftrigonometric%5Fidentities)), they committed a classic translation error known as **"back-translation pseudo-origin" (回译伪源)**: [link](https://www.zhihu.com/question/568998026)

1. They looked at the Chinese word **诱导** (_yòudǎo_).
2. They looked up "诱导" in a general Chinese-English dictionary.
3. The dictionary offered **"induction"** (as in, to induce labor, or to guide).
4. They combined it into **"induction formula"** and printed it in the textbooks. [link](https://www.zhihu.com/question/568998026)

### Summary of the Chain Reaction

```
English: Reduction Formula (Mathematical context)
   ↓
Russian: формулы приведения (Means "Reduction" but also literally "to guide/induce")
   ↓
Chinese: 诱导公式 (Translators picked "to guide/induce" in 1953)
   ↓
English Annotation: Induction Formula (Modern editors translated "诱导" back literally)
```

Because of this linguistic telephone game, a set of formulas meant for **reducing angles** accidentally got a Chinese name meaning **to lure angles**, which was then translated into a fake English math term that means **mathematical induction**. [link](https://www.zhihu.com/question/568998026)
