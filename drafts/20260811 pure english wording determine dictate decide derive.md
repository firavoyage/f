# verbs to describe to make sth derived from it

The exact verb you choose depends entirely on **how** the new thing is being created from the original source.

### Verbs for Material and Physical Creation

Use these when physically changing a raw material or source object into something else:

- **Extract**: To pull a specific substance out of a larger source through a mechanical or chemical process (e.g., _extracting oil from seeds_).
- **Distill**: To purify a liquid by heating it and collecting the vapor, creating a concentrated derivative (e.g., _distilling alcohol from grain_).
- **Forge**: To shape metal using heat and hammers, or metaphorically to create something strong from a base relationship or concept (e.g., _forging steel from iron ore_).
- **Synthesize**: To combine simpler chemical elements or components to form an entirely new substance (e.g., _synthesizing medicine from plant compounds_).
- **Fashion / Mold**: To shape a raw material into a specific form (e.g., _fashioning a bowl from clay_).

### Verbs for Abstract, Intellectual, or Conceptual Creation

Use these when the new thing is an idea, word, or piece of data taken from an existing source:

- **Deduce / Infer**: To arrive at a logical conclusion or fact by analyzing existing information (e.g., _deducing a motive from the evidence_).
- **Extrapolate**: To project, extend, or expand known data or trends into an unknown area to create a new hypothesis (e.g., _extrapolating future sales from past data_).
- **Glean**: To gather small pieces of information or data slowly and patiently from various sources (e.g., _gleaning wisdom from old books_).
- **Coin**: To invent a completely new word or phrase, often by modifying or combining existing words (e.g., _coining a new slang term from an old verb_).

### Phrasal Verbs

- **Made from**: Used when the source material is completely transformed and unrecognizable in the final product (e.g., _Paper is [made from](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E9OjmeMxoPg) wood_).
- **Made out of**: Used when an existing object is upcycled, repurposed, or visibly converted into something else (e.g., _A wallet made out of an old tire_).
- **Stem from / Spring from**: Used to show that an abstract effect, feeling, or issue grew directly out of a root cause (e.g., _His confidence [stems from](https://www.thesaurus.com/browse/derive-from) years of practice_). [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E9OjmeMxoPg)

## can i say a variable derives another

No, you cannot say "a variable derives another" if you mean that changing the first variable automatically determines or calculates the second one. In English, the active form of the verb means _to obtain_, _to trace_, or _to deduce_ something. A variable cannot perform these actions because it is an object, not a thinking agent or a process.

To express this concept correctly, you must use different phrasing depending on your specific field.

### In Mathematics and Programming

If Variable A calculates, creates, or determines Variable B, use these phrases instead:

- Variable B **is derived from** Variable A. _(Passive voice)_
- Variable A **determines** Variable B.
- Variable B **depends on** Variable A.
- Variable A **generates** Variable B.

### In Calculus (Differentiation)

If Variable B is the literal mathematical derivative (dy/dx) of Variable A, use these phrases:

- We **derive** Variable B **from** Variable A. _(You are the one doing the deriving)_
- Variable B **is the derivative of** Variable A.

### Examples in Context

- **Incorrect:** "The total cost variable derives the tax amount."
- **Correct:** "The tax amount variable **is derived from** the total cost."
- **Correct:** "The total cost variable **determines** the tax amount."
