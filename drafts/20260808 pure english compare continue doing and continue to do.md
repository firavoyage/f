compare continue doing and continue to do

In most everyday contexts, <mark><strong>"continue doing" and "continue to do" mean the exact same thing</strong><span> </span>and can be used interchangeably</mark>.

However, there are subtle differences in nuance, grammar rules, and rhythm that native speakers use unconsciously. [link](https://www.italki.com/en/post/question-440724)

### Quick Comparison

| Feature           | Continue Doing (Gerund)                                     | Continue To Do (Infinitive)                               |
| ----------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Primary Focus** | The ongoing process or habit.                               | The habit, effort, or a repeated action.                  |
| **Formal Tone**   | More casual and common in speech.                           | Slightly more formal or academic.                         |
| **Grammar Trap**  | Cannot be used if "continue" is already in the "-ing" form. | The preferred choice if "continue" is in the "-ing" form. |

***

### Key Differences in Nuance

#### 1. Ongoing Process vs. Habitual Actions

- **Continue doing** often highlights a fluid, uninterrupted action that is currently in progress.
  - _Example_: "Please **continue reading** your book." (Keep doing it right now).
- **Continue to do** often highlights a state of being, a recurring habit, or a deliberate effort over time.
  - _Example_: "He **continues to read** a book every week." (A repeated, ongoing habit). [link](https://www.quora.com/Is-continue-to-do-something-different-from-continue-doing-something)

#### 2. The "-ing" Grammar Exception (Double-Ing Rule)

You should **never** use two "-ing" verbs right next to each other because it sounds awkward to native speakers. If you use "continuing" in the present continuous tense, you must follow it with "to do". [link](https://www.italki.com/en/post/question-440724)

- ❌ _Incorrect_: "The economy is **continuing growing**."
- _Correct_: "The economy is **continuing to grow**."

#### 3. Verbs of State vs. Verbs of Action

If you are talking about a state of being or understanding (stative verbs like _know, understand, believe, exist_), English speakers almost exclusively use **continue to do**.

- ❌ _Awkward_: "They **continue knowing** the truth."
- _Correct_: "They **continue to know** the truth."

***

### What about a pause or interruption?

In some regional English textbooks (particularly in parts of Asia), students are taught a strict rule: "continue to do" means resuming an action after a break, while "continue doing" means doing something without stopping.

In real life, **native speakers do not make this distinction**. You can use either phrase whether you took a break or not.

- _Example_: "After lunch, we **continued working**."
- _Example_: "After lunch, we **continued to work**." [link](https://www.tiktok.com/@the%5Fenglishera/video/7028061494843542785)
