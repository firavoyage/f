### more simply or simplier

**"Simplier" is not a real word**, while **"more simply"** is a grammatically correct adverbial phrase. Depending on what you are trying to describe, you should use either **"simpler"** or **"more simply"**.

#### When to use "Simpler" (Adjective)

Use **simpler** to describe a noun (a person, place, or thing). Note the spelling drops the "i" after the "l."

* **Meaning.** Less complicated or easier to do.
* **Examples.** "This math problem is **simpler** than the last one." / "I want a **simpler** life."
* **Note on "more simple."** While grammatically acceptable, grammar guides like [Magoosh](https://magoosh.com/english-speaking/simpler-or-more-simple/) note that **simpler** is preferred because it is shorter and sounds more natural.

#### When to use "More Simply" (Adverb)

Use **more simply** to describe a verb (an action) or how something is done.

* **Meaning.** In an easier, clearer, or less complicated way.
* **Examples.** "Could you explain that **more simply**?" / "She decided to live **more simply** after retiring."

### can you use simpler as adv in casual lang

 \[Highlight start\] **Yes, in casual spoken English, people often use "simpler" as an adverb** \[Highlight end\] , even though it is technically incorrect in formal grammar.

In everyday conversation, native speakers frequently drop the "-ly" from adverbs (these are known as "flat adverbs," like saying "drive safe" instead of "drive safely").

#### How it looks in practice

* **Casual Speech:** "Can you put that **simpler**?" or "Let's try to live **simpler**." (Very common and sounds completely natural in conversation).
* **Formal Writing:** "Can you put that **more simply**?" or "Let's try to live **more simply**." (Required for essays, emails, and professional writing).

#### A Quick Workaround

If "more simply" feels too stiff for a casual chat, you can easily rephrase the sentence to use **simpler** as a standard adjective:

* Instead of _"Explain it more simply,"_ say **"Give me a simpler explanation."**
* Instead of _"Put it more simply,"_ say **"Put it in simpler terms."**
