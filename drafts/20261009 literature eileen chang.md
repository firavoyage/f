# all works of zhang ailin

4:06 AM

**Zhang Ailing** (better known to Western readers as **Eileen Chang**) was one of the most influential modern Chinese writers of the 20th century. Her work spans novels, short stories, essays, and screenplays, famously capturing the complex psychological depth of human relationships against the backdrop of changing Chinese societies like wartime Shanghai and Hong Kong. [link](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Zhang-Ailing)

Below is a categorized overview of her primary literary works, including both her landmark mid-century texts and her posthumously released biographical novels.

### Major Novels and Novellas

- **_Aloeswood Incense: The First Brazier_ (第一炉香)** (1943) – Her breakthrough debut novella detailing a young woman's moral descent within Hong Kong’s colonial high society. [link](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Zhang%20Ailing/980343)
- **_The Golden Cangue_ (金锁记)** (1943) – Widely considered one of the masterpieces of mid-century Chinese literature, it tracks the psychological corruption of a woman bound by a bitter, arranged marriage. [link](https://oac.cdlib.org/findaid/ark:/13030/c85m68v2/admin/)
- **_Love in a Fallen City_ (倾城之恋)** (1943) – A brilliant romance where the wartime siege of Hong Kong unexpectedly paves the way for a cynical couple to find genuine connection. [link](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Zhang-Ailing)
- **_Eighteen Springs_ (十八春)** (1950) – Later revised and republished in 1966 as **_Half a Lifelong Romance_ (半生缘)**, this sweeping melodramatic epic captures the tragic obstacles faced by young lovers over two decades. [link](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Zhang-Ailing)
- **_The Rice Sprout Song_ (秧歌)** (1954) – Written originally in English, this stark novel explores rural Chinese life and the hardships of starvation following the communist revolution. [link](https://www.goodreads.com/author/list/13553962.Zhang%5FAiling)
- **_Naked Earth_ (赤地之恋)** (1954) – Another politically charged novel tracking individuals navigating the land reform movements of mainland China. [link](https://www.britannica.com/biography/Zhang-Ailing)
- **_The Rogue of the North_ (怨女)** (1966) – A reimagining and rewrite of _The Golden Cangue_ that she targeted toward an international audience. [link](https://bookscouter.com/author/zhang-ailing)

### Acclaimed Short Stories

- **_Sealed Off_ (封锁)** (1943) – A surreal vignette where a temporary wartime tram blockade allows two strangers to share a fleeting, intimate romance away from societal expectations.
- **_Red Rose, White Rose_ (红玫瑰与白玫瑰)** (1944) – A sharp psychological study dissecting a man's torn desires between his passionate, unrespectable mistress and his dutiful, frigid wife.
- **_Lust, Caution_ (色，戒)** (1979) – A tense espionage thriller detailing a young woman's plot to assassinate a high-ranking political collaborator in occupied Shanghai, which later became a major feature film.
- _Other Notable Stories:_ "Flower Withers" (花凋), "Steamed Osmanthus Flower: Ah Xiao's Unhappy Autumn" (桂花蒸阿小悲秋), and "Stale Mates" (留情). [link](https://www.abebooks.co.uk/Complete-Works-Zhang-Ailing-Red-Rose/8351292964/bd)

### Posthumously Published Autobiographical Novels

**_Little Reunions_ (小团圆)** (Written in the 1970s, published 2009) – A deeply intimate and controversial autobiographical novel that mirrors her real-life relationship with Japanese collaborator Hu Lancheng.

***

**_The Book of Change_ (易经)** and **_The Fall of the Pagoda_ (雷峰塔)** (Published 2010) – Two companion novels originally drafted in English that retell her turbulent childhood and young adult years in Shanghai and Hong Kong. [link](https://www.amazon.com/Books-zhang-ai-ling/s?rh=n%3A283155%2Cp%5F27%3Azhang%2Bai%2Bling)

### Non-Fiction & Translations

- **_Gossip_ (流言)** (1944) – Her definitive collection of personal essays and prose that showcased her unique philosophies on fashion, art, everyday urban life, and modern femininity.
- **_Written on Water_ (惘然记)** – A compilation translation of many of her essential essays.
- **_The Sing-song Girls of Shanghai_ (海上花列传)** – Eileen Chang's acclaimed modern translation of Han Bangqing's 19th-century Wu dialect masterpiece into English and modern Mandarin. [link](https://www.betterworldbooks.com/author/ailing-zhang/5608174)

## what above are not buyable, or currently available in mainland china

Of the works listed above, **two novels are strictly not available for purchase or officially published in mainland China** due to their explicit political themes: [link](http://hx.cnd.org/2023/10/18/%E8%A2%81%E6%96%8C%EF%BC%9A%E5%BC%A0%E7%88%B1%E7%8E%B2%E7%9A%84%E3%80%8A%E8%B5%A4%E5%9C%B0%E4%B9%8B%E6%81%8B%E3%80%8B%E5%92%8C%E3%80%8A%E7%A7%A7%E6%AD%8C%E3%80%8B%E4%B8%BA%E4%BD%95%E5%9C%A8%E5%A4%A7/)

- **_The Rice Sprout Song_ (秧歌)**
- **_Naked Earth_ (赤地之恋)** [link](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Naked%20Earth/1465646)

### Why are they unavailable?

Both novels were written in the early 1950s after Eileen Chang left mainland China for Hong Kong. Commissioned in part under the sponsorship of the United States Information Service (USIS), they directly critique early Communist Party policies. [link](https://brill.com/display/book/9789004730052/BP000023.xml)

_The Rice Sprout Song_ portrays famine and government systemic abuse during the rural land-reform movement.

***

_Naked Earth_ covers the harsh realities of the "Three-anti" political campaigns and the Korean War. [link](https://baike.baidu.com/item/%E8%B5%A4%E5%9C%B0%E4%B9%8B%E6%81%8B/68029)

Because of these sensitive historical and anti-communist themes, mainland-published collections of Eileen Chang’s works (such as those published by Beijing October Arts and Literature Publishing House) consistently **exclude these two titles**. [link](https://baike.baidu.com/en/item/Naked%20Earth/1465646)

### Current Status of Other Works

Aside from those two, **all other works mentioned above are widely available for purchase, heavily studied, and popular in mainland China today**.\
While Chang's entire catalog was forbidden on the mainland from the 1950s through the late 1970s, the Economic Reforms allowed her literary fiction (_Love in a Fallen City_, _The Golden Cangue_, etc.) to be reintroduced to massive acclaim in the 1980s. Even highly controversial or late-discovered texts like _Lust, Caution_ and her raw autobiographical novel _Little Reunions_ have received official mainland Chinese releases. [link](https://aaww.org/a-lifelong-romance-reflections-on-eileen-changs-life-work-and-legacy/)
