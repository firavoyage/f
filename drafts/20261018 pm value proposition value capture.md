# value proposition value capture pm

In **Product Management (PM)**, <mark>balancing a product's<span> </span><strong>value proposition</strong><span> </span>with its<span> </span><strong>value capture</strong><span> </span>strategy represents the ultimate test of a sustainable business model</mark>. While the **value proposition** focuses on the user—defining how a product solves real problems better than any alternative—**value capture** focuses on the business, ensuring the company successfully monetizes a fair portion of that created value. [link](https://www.simon-kucher.com/en/insights/how-value-capture-model-can-improve-business-operations)

Great Product Managers understand that value creation and value capture are two sides of the same coin: you cannot capture value that doesn't exist, but you cannot sustain a product if you fail to capture revenue from the value you deliver. [link](https://www.svpg.com/value-creation-vs-value-capture/)

***

### The PM's Value Framework

To build a healthy product, a PM must navigate the transition from identifying user needs to securing company margins. [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/ashmaurya%5Fa-business-model-describes-how-a-company-activity-7404151623029055489-e%5Fjo)

```
[ Value Proposition ]  --->  [ Value Delivery ]  --->  [ Value Capture ]
  (Solve the problem)          (Build the features)      (Monetize the solution)
```

| Dimension        | **Value Proposition** (The Promise)                                                                               | **Value Capture** (The Reward)                                                                                    |
| ---------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Core Focus**   | **User-centric**: Focuses on jobs-to-be-done, customer pain points, and product differentiation.                  | **Business-centric**: Focuses on pricing models, monetization mechanics, and revenue retention.                   |
| **Key Question** | _"Why should a customer care about or use this product over a competitor?"_                                       | _"How does the business generate revenue and margin from this utility?"_                                          |
| **PM Artifacts** | Value Proposition Canvas, Persona Maps, PRDs, Positioning Statements.                                             | Pricing tiers, packaging plans, Cost Structures, Unit Economics.                                                  |
| **Failure Mode** | **"A solution in search of a problem"**—building something technologically impressive that nobody actually wants. | **"The Charity Product"**—users love it and rely on it daily, but the business burns cash and cannot monetize it. |

### 1. Mastering the Value Proposition

A strong product value proposition isn't marketing fluff or billboard copy; it is a structural guide for your engineering, design, and roadmap priorities. A standard PM framework relies on answering six critical narrative elements: [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/pawel-huryn%5Fvalue-proposition-is-an-essential-term-for-activity-7268949780863569921-VzcH)

- **The Target Persona:** Exactly _who_ suffers from this friction?
- **Jobs to be Done (JTBD):** What underlying objective is the user trying to achieve?
- **Current State / Friction:** How painful, manual, or expensive is their current workaround (e.g., managing a massive inventory via brittle Excel sheets)?
- **The Capabilities:** The specific features that eliminate that pain.
- **The "After" State:** The quantitative or emotional outcome achieved (e.g., 20% faster developer velocity, peace of mind).
- **The Alternative:** Why your method defeats market substitutes and competitors. [link](https://www.aakashg.com/examples-of-value-proposition/)

### 2. Executing Value Capture

Many companies suffer from **undermonetization**. They build fantastic features but fail to structure their business models to extract a return. For PMs, value capture means engineering the core mechanics of how the product exchanges its value for money: [link](https://publications.pricingsociety.com/value-creation-vs-value-capture/)

- **Pricing Metrics Alignment:** Aligning how you charge with how the user experiences value. For an infrastructure tool, this could be data ingested. For a CRM, it could be per active seat.
- **Packaging and Tiers:** Determining which value-proposition elements belong in a free/basic tier to drive acquisition, and which high-value, "must-have" features belong in enterprise or premium tiers to drive expansion.
- **Price Realization & Execution:** Preventing "value leakage" by working closely with product marketing and sales to ensure discounts don't erode the product's calculated worth. [link](https://www.simon-kucher.com/en/insights/how-value-capture-model-can-improve-business-operations)

### 3. The Balanced PM Rule of Thumb

According to industry benchmarks like the [Silicon Valley Product Group (SVPG)](https://www.svpg.com/value-creation-vs-value-capture/), cross-functional product teams should aim to spend **at least 75% of their time on value creation** (strengthening the value proposition and user experience). Over-indexing on value capture—such as endless paywall optimizations, minor pricing tests, and aggressive monetization walls—delivers only short-term incremental gains and eventually causes user churn. Maximizing true underlying value inherently makes value capture easier down the line. [link](https://www.svpg.com/value-creation-vs-value-capture/)
