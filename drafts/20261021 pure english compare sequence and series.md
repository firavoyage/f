# compare series and sequence

In mathematics, <mark>a<span> </span><strong>sequence is an ordered list of numbers</strong>, while a<span> </span><strong>series is the sum of the terms</strong><span> </span>in a sequence</mark>.

### Quick Comparison

| Feature          | **Sequence**                            | **Series**                          |
| ---------------- | --------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------- |
| **Definition**   | An ordered list of elements or numbers. | The sum of the terms of a sequence. |
| **Separated By** | Commas (e.g., 1,2,3,4)                  | Plus signs (e.g., 1+2+3+4)          |
| **Key Question** | What is the next term in the pattern?   | What do all these terms add up to?  |
| **Notation**     | Written as {𝑎𝑛} or 𝑎1,𝑎2,𝑎3,…           | Written using sigma notation: ∑𝑎𝑛   |
| **Example**      | 2,4,6,8,…                               | 2+4+6+8+…                           |

***

### Key Differences

#### 1. What is a Sequence?

A sequence is a string of numbers that follow a specific rule or pattern. The order of the numbers matters. Each individual number in a sequence is called a **term**.

- **Finite Sequence:** Has a fixed end point (e.g.,\
  5\
  ,\
  10\
  ,\
  15\
  ,\
  20\
  ).
- **Infinite Sequence:** Goes on forever (e.g.,\
  1\
  ,\
  12\
  ,\
  14\
  ,\
  18\
  ,\
  …\
  ).

#### 2. What is a Series?

A series is what you get when you add up all the terms of a sequence.

- **Finite Series:** Has a definitive, calculable total sum.
- **Infinite Series:** Adds up an endless list of numbers. Depending on the behavior of the terms, an infinite series will either **converge** to a specific, finite number (like\
  1

+12

+14

+…\
\=2\
) or **diverge** to infinity.
