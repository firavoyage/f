compare how does sth look and what does sth look like

The core difference is that **"How does it look?"** asks for an opinion, quality evaluation, or temporary condition, while **"What does it look like?"** requests a permanent physical description or a direct comparison to another object. [link](https://www.tiktok.com/@bbclearningenglish/video/7286773982752279841?lang=en)

| Question                              | What it focuses on                              | Expected Answer Type                                       | Example Scenario                                       |
| ------------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------ |
| **How does \[something] look?**       | **Condition, quality, or emotional state**      | **Adjectives** (e.g., _good, broken, sad, clean_)          | Asking a friend for their opinion on your new haircut. |
| **What does \[something] look like?** | **Physical characteristics or visual identity** | **Nouns / Descriptions** (e.g., _tall, blue, like a bird_) | Asking a police officer to describe a missing vehicle. |

### How does it look?

Use this phrase when you want to know someone's judgment or a momentary state. It is answered using **look + adjective**. [link](https://www.talkenglish.com/video/lessondetails.aspx?ALID=4399)

- **Question:** "I just finished painting the kitchen. **How does it look?**"
- **Answer:** "It **looks amazing**! The color is very bright."
- **Question:** "I heard Sarah is sick. **How does she look?**"
- **Answer:** "She **looks tired**, but she is resting." [link](https://www.instagram.com/reel/DTS589Vjb08/?hl=en)

### What does it look like?

Use this phrase when you are unfamiliar with what something is and need an objective description or a comparison to understand it. It is answered using **look like + noun** (or a descriptive sentence). [link](https://www.quora.com/What-is-the-difference-between-What-does-she-look-like-and-how-does-she-look-like)

- **Question:** "I bought a new car today." / "**What does it look like?**"
- **Answer:** "It **looks like a small spaceship**, and it's bright red."
- **Question:** "**What does your brother look like?**"
- **Answer:** "He is **tall with short brown hair**." [link](https://www.facebook.com/PerfectEnglishGrammar/videos/do-we-say-how-does-it-look-like-lets-reviewchoose-what-look-like-or-how-look1-i-/2107074643195106/)

### ⚠️ A Common Mistake to Avoid

Many English learners accidentally combine these two rules and ask: _"How does it look like?"_ [link](https://www.tiktok.com/@grammarslammer/video/7549859339884858646)

This is **grammatically incorrect** in standard English. You must choose one structure or the other: [link](https://www.quora.com/Which-is-grammatically-correct-how-does-it-look-like-or-what-does-it-look-like)

- **Correct:** _What_ does it look _like_?
- **Correct:** _How_ does it look? (No "like" at the end) [link](https://www.quora.com/Which-is-grammatically-correct-how-does-it-look-like-or-what-does-it-look-like)
