why is headquarters plural

The word <mark><strong>"headquarters" is plural in form because it originated as a military compound term combining "head" (meaning chief or commander) and "quarters" (meaning lodging or living spaces)</strong></mark>.

Historically, military camps were divided into sections, and a commander’s assigned living and working area consisted of multiple rooms or tents—hence, plural "quarters". When combined into "headquarters" in the 17th century, the word retained its plural ending to reflect the physical collection of buildings or rooms making up that central hub. [link](https://english.stackexchange.com/questions/54031/are-is-headquarters-always-used-as-plural)

### How to use it correctly

Even though the noun ends in an "-s" and looks plural, modern English treats it as **plural in form but flexible in construction**. [link](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/headquarters)

| Context                                                                                          | Verb Alignment                | Example                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Singular Meaning**Refers to a single main office or central entity.                            | Can take a **singular** verb. | "The company’s **headquarters is** located in New York."                        |
| **Plural Meaning**Refers to the physical rooms, the staff, or multiple distinct corporate sites. | Can take a **plural** verb.   | "The corporation's international **headquarters are** scattered across Europe." |

### The "Headquarter" Exception

You should **never use "headquarter" as a singular noun** (e.g., saying _"I am going to the company headquarter"_ is grammatically incorrect). [link](https://our-languages.canada.ca/en/writing-tips-plus/headquarter-headquarters-headquartered)

The only time you drop the "s" is when using the word as a **verb**: [link](https://www.linkedin.com/posts/proofed%5Fits-both-as-a-noun-headquarters-seems-activity-7125844379046424576--ssN)

- _Correct:_ "We decided to **headquarter** our operations in Tokyo."
- _Correct:_ "The tech giant is **headquartered** in California." [link](https://www.facebook.com/groups/188751454462/posts/10154263433684463/)
