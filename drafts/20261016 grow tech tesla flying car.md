## what do ppl say on the tech

To achieve the physics-defying stunt of driving upside down on a curved ramp, Tesla introduced a **multi-mode fan and ground-sealing system** on the chassis. [link](https://www.euronews.com/2026/10/16/well-it-worked-elon-musk-unveils-new-tesla-roadster-that-can-fly)

- **What it does:** It pulls air out from beneath the car, creating a vacuum that generates **up to 2 Gs of downward force**.
- **The Reaction:** Track day enthusiasts are genuinely excited about this. Experts note that generating artificial downforce mechanically—completely independent of the car's physical tires or standard aerodynamics—could give it unparalleled cornering traction. However, critics mockingly pointed out Elon Musk's choice of words during the livestream, when he jokingly boasted that ["this car really sucks"](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Fdhwkap2DFU).

### 2. SpaceX Cold-Gas Thrusters (The "Rocket" Pack)

Instead of a traditional burning rocket, the aerial maneuvers and the sub-one-second acceleration are powered by **10 high-pressure cold-gas thruster nozzles** hidden around the car. [link](https://tech-insider.org/tesla-roadster-flight-tech-80kn-thrust-explained-2026/)

- **The Tech:** It uses an ultra-high-pressure composite-overwrapped pressure vessel (COPV)—essentially a massive, advanced storage tank located where the back seats used to be—holding compressed air or nitrogen. When activated, it dumps air through the nozzles at the speed of sound. It utilizes all-electric actuators to make precise "micro-adjustments" within fractions of a second to stabilize the car in midair. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=4bbfRQDC6cs)
- **The Reaction:** People are staggered by the raw math. Tech reviewers point out that at maximum output, the system expels roughly **250 kg of air**, generating more than **80 kilonewtons of force**—the raw thrust equivalent of an F-18 fighter jet engine. While the brute-force physics are impressive, people are calling the implementation absurdly impractical, noting that the massive air tank entirely eliminates the car's storage and rear seating. [link](https://www.motortrend.com/news/tesla-roadster-thrust-system-spacex-technology-explained)

### 3. Steer-by-Wire & Minimalist Tech Cabin

Tesla engineers confirmed the car completely ditches physical connections for a total **steer-by-wire yoke system**, alongside wire-controlled brakes and throttle. The seats are also molded directly into a ultra-lightweight magnesium, titanium, and carbon fiber chassis. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=QTLY1CigV7w)

- **The Reaction:** Purist sports car drivers are deeply suspicious of "by-wire" steering systems, fearing a lack of road feel, though Tesla claims it's automated dynamically to handle the extreme forces of track driving.

Overall, the consensus on the technology is that Tesla successfully built an incredibly advanced, rocket-assisted **stunt drone**. However, the general public views it as a "science experiment" rather than consumer tech, since the hardware required to make the car fly ruins almost every practical element of a road car. ## how do ppl judge their tech

The public and engineering experts judge the technology behind the new Tesla Roadster by dividing it into two distinct categories: **brilliant mechanical physics** and **terrible consumer utility**.

While nobody denies that the engineering required to pull off the live stunt is a massive milestone, people are analyzing the tech using strict mathematical equations and real-world viability filters. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fyqY%5Fa9ri0)

### 1. The Physics and Math Actually "Check Out"

When engineers and data analysts look at the raw numbers, they generally agree that **Tesla's performance claims are scientifically possible**.

- **The Acceleration Formula:** To achieve the claimed 0-to-60 mph time of **0.81 seconds** (or under 0.59 seconds on the top-tier Rocket trim), a vehicle needs to pull roughly **4.6 Gs of force**. Standard rubber tires physically lose grip and max out around 1.5 Gs. [link](https://cellcog.ai/blog/tesla-roadster-physics/)
- **The Verdict:** Experts writing for publications like [CellCog](https://cellcog.ai/blog/tesla-roadster-physics/) ran the numbers on the 600-liter carbon-overwrapped pressure vessels (COPV) pressurized to 5,500 psi. They confirmed that dumping 250 kg of compressed air through the **80 kilonewton thruster system** bridges that mathematical gap perfectly. The math works; the thrusters genuinely override the physical limits of tire traction. [link](https://tech-insider.org/tesla-roadster-flight-tech-80kn-thrust-explained-2026/)

### 2. A Masterclass in Aerospace Synergy

Aerospace and drone engineers give Tesla’s engineering team immense credit for how the car behaves in midair. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=3fyqY%5Fa9ri0)

- Instead of just launching a heavy object into the air like a dumb stunt ramp, the Roadster relies on an **all-electric reaction control layout** identical to SpaceX rockets. Four main thrusters handle brute propulsion, while smaller, millisecond-accurate attitude thrusters handle rotation. [link](https://tech-insider.org/tesla-roadster-flight-tech-80kn-thrust-explained-2026/)
- **The Verdict:** Tech reviewers from [MotorTrend](https://www.motortrend.com/news/tesla-roadster-thrust-system-spacex-technology-explained) noted that watching a heavy chassis detect its own orientation, execute a barrel roll, and dynamically fire reverse thrusters to cushion its own descent is an **unprecedented automotive computing feat**. [link](https://www.motortrend.com/news/tesla-roadster-thrust-system-spacex-technology-explained)

### 3. The "Suction Fan" is Highly Praised

The underbody multi-mode suction fan system—which generates up to **2 Gs of artificial downforce**—is being judged as the most practical tech on the car.

- **The Verdict:** Unlike the thrusters, track enthusiasts and racing engineers highly respect ground-effect fans (a concept pioneered by the historic Brabham BT46B "Fan Car" and the modern McMurtry Spéirling). Because it creates slot-car-like grip even when the vehicle is moving slowly, experts judge this specific tech as a legitimate weapon that could easily **crush every production track record in existence**.

### 4. Terrible Resource Efficiency for a Consumer Car

Where the technical judgments turn overwhelmingly negative is how the "flying" tech compromises the fundamental engineering of a sports car.

- **The Weight & Space Penalty:** Storing 600 liters of compressed air requires two massive, reinforced tanks. On the "Thrust" trim, this completely deletes the back seats. On the full "Rocket" trim, the hardware takes up so much physical space that the car is forced into a **highly impractical single-seat layout**.
- **The Range Compromise:** True hypercar engineers are pointing out that Tesla originally promised a revolutionary **620-mile range** via a massive 200-kWh battery back in 2017. By pivoting to heavy pneumatic tanks and high-draw suction fans, Tesla had to sacrifice battery capacity. [link](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=c25bVS99j7s)

People judge the tech as an **engineering masterpiece but a commercial gimmick**. Tesla successfully used brilliant aerospace math to solve the physics of a flying car—but in doing so, they engineered a vehicle that is too loud, too dangerous, and too stripped of interior space to ever function as a usable road car. ## why is it engineering masterpiece, to what extent

To understand why engineers call the Roadster an engineering masterpiece, you have to look past the "flying car" gimmick and focus on how Tesla **shattered the absolute physical limits of traditional automotive engineering.**

Tesla didn’t just make a fast car; they integrated aerospace physics with automotive computing in a way that has **never been done before.** Here is the extent to which they pushed the boundaries of technology:

### 1. Breaking the "Tire Grip" Limit (0 to 60 mph in 0.59s)

In traditional car design, there is a hard physical ceiling called the **friction coefficient of rubber on asphalt**. No matter how much horsepower a car has, traditional rubber tires cannot grab the road hard enough to launch a car from 0 to 60 mph in under ~1.7 seconds. The tires will simply spin.

- **To What Extent:** Tesla completely bypassed the limits of Earth's friction by treating the car like a rocket. By applying **80 kilonewtons of directional thrust** via the SpaceX cold-gas system, the car creates its own forward propulsion completely independent of tire traction. It pulls up to **4.6 Gs of force** on launch—more than an astronaut experiences during a Space Shuttle liftoff.

### 2. Microsecond Midair Stabilization

Launching a heavy object into the air is easy; controlling it so it doesn't crash upside down is almost impossible for an automobile.

- **To What Extent:** The Roadster’s computing architecture utilizes an advanced **Reaction Control System (RCS)** cloned directly from Falcon 9 rockets. The car's onboard computers sample its orientation in midair hundreds of times per second. It uses **all-electric actuators** to open and close rocket nozzles within milliseconds, blowing out precisely metered bursts of compressed air to self-correct a roll, flip the car, and cushion its own landing. It is a level of automated, real-time spatial awareness never before seen in a consumer vehicle.

### 3. Decoupling Speed from Aerodynamics (The Fan System)

Normally, a car needs to be moving incredibly fast (usually over 100 mph) for its wings and spoilers to catch the wind and push the car down into the pavement for grip. This means cars are notoriously unstable in slow, sharp corners.

- **To What Extent:** The Roadster's underbody suction fan generates **2 Gs of downward force while standing completely still**. By mechanically sucking the air out from under the chassis to create a literal vacuum, Tesla decoupled downforce from vehicle speed. An engineer can now program the car to have maximum, slot-car-like grip during a 15 mph hairpin turn just as easily as a 200 mph straightaway.

### 4. Severe Extreme-Pressure Engineering

Managing the energy required to lift a car requires dealing with terrifying amounts of pressure.

- **To What Extent:** The car utilizes **Carbon-Overwrapped Pressure Vessels (COPV)** pressurized to **5,500 psi**. To put that in perspective, a standard car tire sits at about 35 psi. Safely containing that much explosive pneumatic energy inside a lightweight, crash-tested consumer vehicle chassis without it turning into a bomb is an incredibly complex structural engineering achievement.

## how is it explored before

While Tesla’s integration of these technologies into a unified computer-controlled system is groundbreaking, the individual core concepts—**suction fans** and **rocket-assisted propulsion**—have a fascinating history of exploration by eccentric engineers and racing teams dating back over 50 years.

Here is how these two technologies were explored before Tesla:

### 1. The Precursors to the Vacuum Fan System

Tesla’s underbody suction tech is a direct evolution of what the racing world calls **"Fan Cars."**

- **The 1970 Chaparral 2J:** Legendary engineer Jim Hall built an experimental Can-Am race car featuring two massive fans powered by a separate snowmobile engine at the back. It sucked air out from under the car, creating massive downforce. It was so fast that rival drivers complained it threw rocks and debris straight at them, leading to an immediate ban.
- **The 1978 Brabham BT46B "Fan Car":** Formula 1 genius Gordon Murray designed a car with a massive fan driven by the main engine clutch. It claimed the fan was just for "cooling," but it actually sucked the car to the ground. Niki Lauda drove it to an effortless victory in its very first race, after which it was instantly banned because it made cornering speeds unsafely high.
- **The 2021 McMurtry Spéirling:** This modern British electric track car proved the tech worked for EVs. It uses a massive twin-fan system to generate 4,400 lbs of downforce at a standstill. In 2022, it shattered the historic Goodwood Hill Climb record, proving that vacuum suction completely annihilates traditional aerodynamics.

### 2. The Precursors to Rocket-Assisted Cars

Using thrust to move wheeled vehicles has been explored since the early 20th century, though usually for straight-line speed rather than acrobatics.

- **The 1928 Opel RAK2:** Fritz von Opel (the "Rocket Fritz") bolted 24 solid-fuel rockets to the back of a primitive race car. It reached a mind-boggling 143 mph in Germany before the dangerous project was shut down due to the extreme risk of explosions.
- **Cold-Gas Thruster Stunts:** Prior to Tesla, high-pressure cold-gas thrusters (like the ones Tesla uses) were strictly the domain of aerospace companies like NASA and SpaceX for maneuvering satellites and rockets in the vacuum of space. The only times they were put on land vehicles were for specialized lunar rover simulations or hyper-specific Hollywood movie stunt rigs.
- **The "Reaction Control" Missing Link:** No one had ever successfully wired space-grade Reaction Control thrusters to an automotive stability control computer.
